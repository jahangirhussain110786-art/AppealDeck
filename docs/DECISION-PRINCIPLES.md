# Decision principles

Working rules for anyone — human or AI — making calls on AppealDeck. Written 21 September 2026 after four research phases showed that good process had been substituting for commercial judgement.

These are not policy. They are how to think. If one of them conflicts with a locked decision, say so out loud and let the founder choose; do not quietly follow either one.

---

## 1. Check the code, not the document

This project's documents have repeatedly described a product the source did not contain: a composer that "drafted" bracketed placeholders, reminders that were "tracked" but never sent, routing that was "in place" for cases it silently refused.

**Apply it:** before repeating any claim about what the product does, open the file. Cite `path:line`. A planning document is a record of intent, not of behaviour.

## 2. A locked decision is a hypothesis with a date on it

D1–D10 were good calls with the evidence available in August. By September, one of the facts under them had already moved: Amazon's own assistant began guiding appeals, which is the thing the free-decode wedge was built to own.

**Apply it:** treat every decision as carrying an expiry. When the world moves under one, say so plainly and name what changed. Locked means "do not reopen casually," not "do not notice."

## 3. Separate a decision from its implementation

D6 gates three categories: forged documents, fraud, child safety. The router excludes nine — it also refuses intellectual property, trademark, copyright, related accounts, product safety, recalls, identity verification and frozen funds. Six of those were never decided by anyone. They accumulated.

**Apply it:** when something looks locked, find the sentence that locked it. If no sentence exists, it is not a decision — it is drift, and it can be changed without ceremony.

## 4. Follow the money before the feature

Four phases produced a journey map, a capability table and a pilot plan without once calculating what a customer costs to acquire or what one is worth. That single number turned out to constrain the strategy more than every feature decision combined.

**Apply it:** for any plan that intends to make money, get the unit economics on the table early — price, net of fees, cost to acquire, how often the customer comes back. If the arithmetic does not work, no feature fixes it.

## 5. Distribution decides the price, not the reverse

You cannot pick a price and then find a channel. The channel has a cost, and that cost sets the floor for what the price must be. A price too low for its channel is not "affordable" — it is unsellable, because nothing can carry it to a buyer.

**Apply it:** name the channel and its cost before defending a price.

## 6. Price is a claim about quality, not just a number

In high-stakes distress purchases, a low price can read as low competence. Someone losing $10,000 a month does not choose the cheapest option; they choose the one they believe will work. Cheap is only persuasive when the buyer already trusts you.

**Apply it:** ask what the price *says* before asking whether it is affordable.

## 7. Build what the platform structurally cannot

Amazon will happily explain its own policies, and is already doing it for free. Amazon will never build a tool that helps a seller argue against Amazon, keeps an independent record of what Amazon asked, or shows that Amazon's last three replies were identical boilerplate.

**Apply it:** when choosing what to build, prefer the capability that is adversarial to the platform's interests. That is the part that cannot be taken away.

## 8. Respect the evidence hierarchy, and never let a summary become a rule

Primary source beats platform staff beats vendor marketing beats forum anecdote beats AI summary. This project nearly hardened several invented figures — rejection percentages, word counts, bot-scoring — into product behaviour because an AI transcript stated them confidently.

**Apply it:** a number with no traceable source does not enter the product, the copy, or the plan. "Unverified" is a finding, not a gap to fill.

## 9. Effort spent is not evidence of value

Five visual passes, thirty-four definition-of-done items and twenty-two amendments have been completed. Zero suspended sellers have used the product. The work is real; none of it is validation.

**Apply it:** measure progress in things learned from outside the building, not in tasks closed inside it.

## 10. Every week without a user compounds the wrong assumptions

Building longer before contact does not reduce risk; it increases the amount of work built on top of unverified beliefs.

**Apply it:** when choosing between shipping something small and building something complete, prefer the one that produces a real reaction from a real seller sooner.

## 11. Honesty is an attacking position

In a market whose defining feature is fake success rates and bribery convictions, honesty is not a constraint to be worked around — it is the only differentiation available to an unknown founder. But it must be phrased as a capability, not an apology. "We cannot guarantee reinstatement" is a disclaimer. "We show you what is missing before Amazon does" is a product.

**Apply it:** turn every honest limitation into the active thing it lets you claim.

## 12. Name the uncomfortable thing first

The most useful outputs of these four phases were the unwelcome ones: that the product refuses the cases sellers care most about, that nobody has been observed paying this price shape, that a case file can be silently deleted while the seller waits.

**Apply it:** lead with the finding that costs something. If an analysis contains no bad news, it is probably not finished.

## 13. Precision in words is a product feature

Sellers in panic read fast and trust little. Vague words cost conversions and invite refunds. "Appeal help" says nothing. "Never send Amazon the same appeal twice" says exactly one thing, is verifiable, and promises nothing untrue.

**Apply it:** prefer the sentence that could be falsified. If a claim could not possibly be wrong, it is not saying anything.

## 14. Serve the person who already has the customers

Reaching scattered, panicking individuals is the hardest distribution problem there is. Whoever already has their trust — a consultant, a community, an agency — is a shorter path to revenue than any advertisement.

**Apply it:** before designing an acquisition funnel, ask who already has this audience and what they need.

## 15. Separate what protects the business from what merely constrains it

Refusing fraud cases protects the business: it is the chargeback defence, the payment-processor defence and the trust position all at once. Refusing intellectual-property cases protects nothing — it just forfeits the market. Both felt like "ethics."

**Apply it:** for each constraint, name the concrete harm it prevents. If none can be named, it is a preference, and preferences are negotiable.
