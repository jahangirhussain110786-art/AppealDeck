# The AI-era angle: are our prices too high? (22 Oct 2026)

> **What this is.** The founder's third question on 22 Oct 2026: in a world where sellers can ask ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini for free, are the planned prices too high at launch, and what makes a seller choose AppealDeck and stay? Two research agents (how sellers use general AI for appeals; how AI-era vertical tools price against free chatbots) plus a capability-by-capability comparison against `docs/CURRENT-STATE.md`. Research and judgement only; nothing built. This is input to the combined plan the founder asked for next, alongside `2026-10-22-pricing-strategy-research.md` and `2026-10-22-growth-and-pricing-blueprint.md`. ## 0. The answer

- **$249 is not too high for what it is.** Humans charge $500 to $3,000 per appeal (V); the stakes are held money and a closed account.
- **$249 is too high as the first thing a stranger is asked to pay**, in a market where **a letter is now worth $0**: AppealsPro's free tier writes letters and keeps a case, a 786-star free Claude skill drafts POAs, SellerForge gives a free template, and ChatGPT costs $0 to $20 (V).
- **So the problem is not the number; it is what we charge for and where the first payment sits.** Charge for what a chatbot cannot do (checking the seller's documents against what Amazon asked, remembering every round, refusing to invent facts). Never charge for "a letter".
- **Launch shape:** keep $249 as the Case Pass; add a **$49 first step** (every document checked against the notice), credited in full to the Pass; give away everything that explains. Re-test after 20 to 50 paid cases.
- **Professional prices from this morning's blueprint are too high** against AppealsPro ($79.99 a month for about 100 letters, V). They need to come down to about **$29 to $39 per case**, fenced by a minimum pack size instead of by price (§5).

## 1. The substitute is real, growing and unmeasured

- About half of Amazon sellers used AI for their business in 2024 (Jungle Scout, ~2,000 sellers, V). No survey measures AI use for appeals (V, searched).
- A former Amazon investigator reports "maybe an uptick of 10, 20, 30%" more sellers writing appeals with ChatGPT than a year ago: an impression, not a measurement (ecommerceChris, V).
- The agencies are publishing rebuttals ("Don't let ChatGPT write it for you", Riverbend's "ChatGPT, Claude, and Amazon Seller Appeals", several lawyer videos; V/S). People rarely argue against a substitute nobody uses.
- **Amazon itself is moving closer:** Seller Assistant has 230,000+ monthly users (V), and a Seller Assistant plugin for Claude went into US beta on 23 Sep 2026 with live performance metrics and "preparing Seller Support cases" (V). It does not mention appeals yet. **This is the substitute to watch.**

## 2. What goes wrong when a chatbot writes the appeal

From people who sell human appeal services (V on each page; treat as interested parties):

- "AI can arrange the facts you give it, but it can't tell you if you've provided the right ones" (Riverbend).
- Polished but generic: "A template dressed in better vocabulary is still just a template."
- Outdated standards pulled from the open web; invented evidence ("hallucinated"); too long; resubmitting the same weak letter; documents that do not match the account.
- An Amazon staff reply on the forum: "an appeal alone will not be accepted"; the seller needed a document (V). **The documents decide, not the prose.**
- **"Amazon rejects AI-written appeals" is unproven.** Only consultants claim it; no Amazon statement exists (V, searched). Never use it as a selling line (FTC v. DoNotPay standard). The defensible line: generic text that ignores what the notice asked fails, whoever wrote it.

**The one-sentence position:** _ChatGPT writes the letter. AppealDeck checks the proof._ The chatbot's weakness is not prose; it cannot tell the seller that the invoice is dated outside 365 days, that the supplier name does not match, or that the units fall 120 short. Those are what refusals are made of.

## 4. How AI-era tools price (the evidence)

- **The closest analog, insurance-denial appeals:** Claimable $39.95 per appeal plus shipping and charges for delivery, tracking and reminders; Fight Health Insurance free / pay-what-you-want; Counterforce Health free, grant-funded (V). **The letter is free or under $40; tools charge for process.**
- **Consumer AI spend:** in Aug 2026 only 4.5% of US consumers paid for ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude; the median payer spends $25 a month (a16z, V). That is the anchor a stranger brings.
- **Vertical AI defends price by selling outcomes or tasks, not access** (Bessemer, Feb 2026, V): a unit the buyer recognizes (per resolution, per case), workflow and record-keeping that are hard to copy.
- **Free beats cheap:** cutting to $0 pulls much harder than any small price (Shampanier, Mazar, Ariely, S). A $9 "tripwire" loses the pull of free and adds friction; no good study shows it raises buyers (U).
- **A lower first price helps strangers** when there is a second purchase to come (Anderson and Simester, field tests on ~600k catalog customers, S). Here the second purchase is the Pass, so a credited first step fits.
- **Seller tool budgets:** $29 to $129 a month is normal (Helium 10, Jungle Scout, V/S). A $249 Pass is two to eight months of a seller's main tool, which feels large unless the value is seen first.

**Effect on a seller's path (E):** decode free → one document check free shows a real finding → $49 checks all documents → $200 more for the Pass (total $249). The first payment is in the band a stranger already pays for AI; the full price is reached after the value is seen.

## 6. Reasons to choose AppealDeck and stay, in the AI era

1. **It checks the proof**, not just the wording (§3).
2. **It remembers**: every round, every reply, every document, with "what changed" before a resubmission.
3. **It never adds a fact you did not give it**: the fact lock and the "Your facts, checked" stamp.
4. **It tells you what Amazon asked for**, including the records the notice did not name.
5. **It keeps your case on your device**, and never logs in to your Amazon account.
6. **It warns you about fake notices** before you answer one.
7. **One price per case, every round**: no tokens, no monthly meter.
8. **For professionals:** the same checks on every client's case, client names on exports, $29 to $39 a case.

**Not reasons we can give:** "Amazon rejects ChatGPT appeals" (unproven), any win rate (no data), "better than a lawyer" (FTC).

## 7. Risks this angle adds

- **Amazon's Claude plugin** gaining appeal preparation with live account data. If it does, the remaining edge is the document checks, the round-by-round record and the fact lock: keep investing there, not in prose.
- **AppealsPro adding document checks.** Watch its pricing page monthly.
- **Seller voice is thin.** Before the combined plan is final, the founder reads 20 recent "I used ChatGPT and got refused" posts in the Facebook and Reddit groups the agents could not reach, and notes what each seller says went wrong.
