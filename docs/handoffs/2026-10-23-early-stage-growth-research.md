# Early-stage growth in 2026: what the evidence says, and what it means for AppealDeck (23 Oct 2026)

Done directly (web search plus reading pages; no sub-agents). Labels: **PRIMARY** (a report with its method shown), **VENDOR** (a company that sells the thing it measures; treat as direction, not fact), **OPINION** (a practitioner blog), **NONE** (looked and found nothing). The books named earlier (Hormozi and others) are not available to me as text; their frameworks are applied in `2026-10-22-combined-growth-plan.md`, and this file does not repeat them.

## What the evidence says

1. **First customers come from the founder talking to a narrow group, not from ads or content.** Every guide agrees: pick a narrow buyer, contact 50 of them personally, sell by hand first, ask each buyer for a referral, avoid paid ads and affiliate programs until conversion is proven. [Causo channel mix](https://hub.causo.ai/guides/first-100-paying-customers-channel-mix-2026), [Under30CEO](https://www.under30ceo.com/how-to-find-your-first-100-customers/), [Supademo](https://supademo.com/blog/saas-customer-acquisition-playbook) (all OPINION or VENDOR; the one channel split with numbers has no method shown). Early customers also tend to be people already comparing options, so "alternative to X" and "X versus Y" pages catch buyers at the right moment. This matches the existing plan (writers and VAs first, no ads).
2. **Free-to-paid conversion varies tenfold, so a benchmark tells us little.** Self-serve B2B freemium sits mostly at 3 to 8 percent of signed-up free users, with the best at 10 to 15 and a quarter of products under 2.5 ([ChartMogul via Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/freemium-to-premium), secondary write-ups; the primary report did not load). It measures accounts, not anonymous visitors, and none of it covers a one-time purchase made in a crisis. **Do not plan on a number; measure our own funnel.**
3. **Cheap subscriptions are the worst-retained products of the AI era.** [ChartMogul and Kyle Poyar, Dec 2025](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-retention-the-ai-churn-wave/) (PRIMARY: about 200 AI-native companies, 3,500 scanned in all, only companies past $250k ARR): AI products under $50 a month kept 23 percent of revenue over a year, $50 to $249 kept 45, over $250 kept 70 (the same as ordinary B2B software). Its explanation: easy to buy means easy to cancel, and buyers see themselves as trying it. **We sell one-time, per case, at $99 to $249, and the plan's Pro plan is $349 a month: all in the stable band. A cheap monthly plan is the wrong move.**
4. **Interactive free tools convert far better than forms, and automated emails far better than campaigns.** A tool that gives value first and asks for an email second is the standard pattern, and the advice is to skip account creation for the free tool ([free-tool strategy guide](https://tessl.io/registry/skills/github/popey/claude-code-skills/free-tool-strategy), VENDOR; the "30 to 50 percent" figure is unsourced). Automated and transactional email averages about 31 percent opens and 7 percent clicks against 21 and 2 for campaigns ([Brevo](https://www.brevo.com/blog/email-marketing-benchmarks/), VENDOR; opens are inflated by Apple's mail privacy). A deadline reminder is that kind of email.
5. **Referred customers stay longer, and few partners ever refer.** One bank study cited by a referral vendor found 18 percent lower churn for referred customers (VENDOR). Of affiliates, about 7.6 percent generate any referral, and median activation is around 18 percent ([GrowSurf](https://growsurf.com/statistics/), [Fungies](https://fungies.io/saas-referral-program-guide), VENDOR). Commissions of 20 to 30 percent are normal for subscriptions. **NONE found for one-time products.** Our 30 percent one-time commission is in range; expect to recruit twenty to get one or two active.
6. **In high-stakes use, people trust a tool that explains itself, but not one that buries them in detail.** Participants preferred a slightly less accurate system that gave reasons; overly technical disclosures reduced trust and use; trust falls where AI decides without human review ([Zenodo, Feb 2026](https://zenodo.org/records/18641697), [HEC Paris](https://www.hec.edu/en/knowledge/articles/what-extent-do-people-follow-algorithms-advice-more-human-advice)). No study links this to purchases. It supports what the product does (the seller confirms every fact; short reasons) and warns against more disclosure text.
7. **Pricing pages: three tiers, one highlighted plan, mobile first.** One vendor reports 41 percent better conversion for three tiers over four or more (4,200 tests, no method shown: [Visionary Marketing](https://visionary-marketing.co.uk/blog/pricing-page-conversion-statistics-2026)); the guidance elsewhere agrees but is OPINION. **Our sellers' page now shows four cards, two of them disabled "Opens soon".**
8. **Being found by AI answer engines is the same work as ordinary search:** direct, structured answers to specific questions on a narrow topic. Nothing found shows that original data raises citations ([LLMPulse](https://llmpulse.ai/blog/geo-guide/), [HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/generative-engine-optimization-small-business), VENDOR).
9. **Nothing reliable on market size.** "22 to 35 percent of sellers are suspended" and "2,000 a month" are unsourced. Do not use them.
10. **Nothing found** on how products bought once in a crisis keep customers, and nothing on Pakistani sellers specifically. The honest model for us is: retention equals the next round of the same case, the next notice on the same account, and referrals.

## What it means for AppealDeck

**Do now, no new features (config and copy):**
- **Hide "Opens soon" cards on the sellers' page at launch** (`NEXT_PUBLIC_PLANS_PREVIEW=off`, which `docs/DEPLOYMENT.md` currently says to leave unset). A buyer sees Free, Appeal Pass and, once priced, One Response, not two dead buttons. Keep the preview on the writers' and partners' pages if you want them to see what is coming. This is the one finding that is a single setting.
- **Add comparison and alternative pages** for the questions buyers type ("ChatGPT for Amazon appeals", "alternative to AppealsPro"), factual and dated, no win rates. The research files already hold the facts.
- **Measure before opinions:** set `NEXT_PUBLIC_PLAUSIBLE_DOMAIN` so the funnel (decode, intake, checkout opened, purchase) exists from day one.
- **Check AI answers monthly by hand:** ask ChatGPT and Perplexity ten of the questions a suspended seller would ask and record whether we are cited.

**Small builds that need your pick (nothing built):**
- **Opt-in deadline email after a decode** ("email me this checklist and a reminder before the date"). Gives value first, builds the only list we can have, and is the highest-opening kind of email.
- **A referral prompt** after a pack download or a recorded reply, and the partner system you already chose (R-17).

**Do not do:** a cheap monthly plan, paid ads, a published success rate, more tiers, or more disclosure text.

**What nobody can answer for us:** what share of decoders buy. The first 50 cases will, which is why the plan says to wait before building more.
