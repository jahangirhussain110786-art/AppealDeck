# Ask about repeats, past rounds and links

An account's past changes an Amazon appeal mainly through four channels, and the sources support each one: **repeats of the same policy** (Amazon raises the cost of each repeat and caps repeats in a 180-day window), **what earlier rounds already put on the record** (refused letters, escalations used, documents Amazon called unverified or forged), **other accounts tied to this one** (a related account that is still deactivated can block a good letter), and **money and identity checks** that run separately from the appeal. Account age, lifetime sales and the tone of old messages have no sourced role as appeal inputs. That is the honest answer to the founder's line, "same violation, same POA, one account reopens, the other does not": the letter can be the same and the record behind it different, because one account is on its third same-policy warning, has two refused near-copies on file, once sent an invoice Amazon called manipulated, or shares a bank account with a deactivated store. The professional firms whose lists are published ask for exactly those things: the full notice, every earlier submission and Amazon's replies, the case documents, and, for linked cases, who has access and what is shared. For AppealDeck the gap is not the earlier-appeals step, which is built, but the account's own background: earlier warnings, what Amazon said about each document before, other accounts and who can log in, changes already made, and escalation routes already used. None of this should become a score or a chance (product rule D6). It should change what the tool asks, what it checks before sending, and what the letter must cover.

## Most "official" wording here is second-hand, and must be checked signed in

**Seller Central help pages could not be read without a login.** Every help URL the researchers fetched (the Account Health Rating page G521, its FAQ, the Seller Code of Conduct G1801, the multiple-accounts page, the funds policy, the Seller Challenge FAQ, Amazon's own appeal guidance) returned only the logged-out shell. Amazon's wording in this report therefore comes from verbatim reposts (chiefly the Hopted mirror of the Account Health Rating article), posts by Amazon staff accounts on the Seller Forums, Amazon notices that sellers quote on the forums, and search-engine snippets. The one Amazon text read in full is the Business Solutions Agreement (BSA) PDF on Amazon's own CDN, and even that links to a 2022 translation, so whether the November 2026 live wording is unchanged is not confirmed ([Amazon BSA PDF](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/G/01/rainier/help./1.1_BSA_PDF_English.pdf)). **Before any sentence below goes into product copy as "Amazon says", someone signed in to Seller Central must check it against the live page.**

Tags are kept from the research notes: **[V]** is Amazon's own wording, from an Amazon page or a named verbatim repost; **[P]** is a named practitioner's experience; **[U]** is a forum anecdote, an unopened thread, a search snippet or an unattributed claim. Most practitioner sources sell appeal services, which is a reason to weigh their claims rather than discard them.

**No success rates appear in this report.** Where a source gave one (for example a vendor's estimate of how many Code of Conduct suspensions are reinstated, or third-party Account Health point tables), it had no method or Amazon source and is not repeated.

## Repeats of the same policy are the history fact Amazon itself names

The clearest official statement about account history is in the Account Health Rating (AHR) policy. **[V]** The score reflects "policy adherence and selling activities over the last 180 days", new sellers start at 200, and "the point values associated with violations included in your AHR increase each time that policy is violated" ([Hopted mirror of Amazon's AHR article](https://www.hopted.com/integrations/amazon-sp-api/amazon-help-articles/seller-performance/account-health-rating)). **[V]** Separately from the score, an account "may be at immediate risk of deactivation if you reach the maximum number of repeat violations for infringement-related policies or restricted products policies in a 180-day period", and "in most cases" that maximum is **five for infringement-related policies and two for restricted products** ([Amazon Seller Forums post quoting the policy](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/45aed975-7ba9-4c65-9a9f-1ceb589d8014); [Hopted mirror](https://www.hopted.com/integrations/amazon-sp-api/amazon-help-articles/seller-performance/account-health-rating)). The mirror says this applies regardless of the AHR. The bands are green "Healthy" 200 to 1,000, yellow "At Risk" 100 to 199, and red "Unhealthy" at 99 or below, meaning "eligible for deactivation or already deactivated" **[V]** ([Hopted mirror](https://www.hopted.com/integrations/amazon-sp-api/amazon-help-articles/seller-performance/account-health-rating)).

Two practical points follow. First, **a green AHR does not protect against a repeat-cap deactivation**, so a tool must never reassure a seller that a healthy band means low risk. Second, the useful question is not "what is your score?" but "how many warnings for this same policy in the last 180 days, and when?" Amazon's wording ("each time that policy is violated") points to the same policy; whether different policies count together is not stated. Whether violations older than 180 days stay visible to the reviewer who reads an appeal is also unknown: they drop out of the score, but no source says they leave the record. Claims that warnings vanish from the Account Health page after 180 days are **[U]** search snippets only.

Amazon's own notices tend to name the history when they deactivate for it. **[U]** A forum-quoted notice says the account was "previously alerted" that it was "at risk of deactivation due to incurring repeat violations", and that "no valid plan of action had been received" ([Seller Forums UK](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/seller-forums/discussions/t/f4938785-2ef5-4b5d-97af-6e6adc5021d9); thread not opened). **[V]** The only Amazon appeal wording obtained asks for "a plan of action to correct the issues that contributed to the enforcement action" ([Hopted mirror](https://www.hopted.com/integrations/amazon-sp-api/amazon-help-articles/seller-performance/account-health-rating)); the plural is the only official hint that contributing history belongs in the letter.

What a repeat letter must do is practitioner advice, not Amazon rule. **[P]** Chris McCabe (former Amazon investigator) says not to answer warnings generically: analyse the specific root causes behind "the warnings and suspension", and give preventive measures that apply to all inventory, not ASIN by ASIN ([wortfilter.de repost of McCabe, 2016](https://wortfilter.de/amazon-plan-of-action-poa-dos-and-donts-von-chris-mccabe/)). **[P]** A root cause should be "the operational breakdown that triggered the policy issue, not the symptom, and not the metric" ([Amazon Sellers Lawyer](https://amazonsellerslawyer.com/blog/how-to-create-a-strategic-well-written-plan-of-action-to-reinstate-selling-privileges/)). **[P]** The BSA's seven-day cure window after a notified breach, as Riverbend paraphrases Section 3, is a reason to ask whether an earlier warning went unanswered ([Riverbend](https://riverbendconsulting.com/blog/amazon-section-3-violation/)). The well-known phrase "repeated violations may result in permanent deactivation" could not be traced to an Amazon page and must not be quoted as Amazon's.

The other "account background" facts the founder raised have weak or no support. **No Amazon statement says account age or sales history counts in an appeal.** **[P]** McCabe advises keeping performance metrics and Account Health screenshots out of a policy appeal ([wortfilter.de](https://wortfilter.de/amazon-plan-of-action-poa-dos-and-donts-von-chris-mccabe/)). **[U]** A seller claiming over $22 million in sales was still permanently deactivated over alleged forged documents ([Seller Forums](https://sellercentral.amazon.in/seller-forums/discussions/t/b139588b-e961-49f5-aa3c-193f3ea2f1a9)), and a quoted notice names "sales volumes … not supported by buyer feedback or an established sales history" as the trigger, so a young account with a sudden sales jump is its own case type (thread not pinned). On tone, **[P]** practitioners agree that venting, pleading, blaming buyers or commenting on Amazon's slowness correlate with failed appeals ([wortfilter.de](https://wortfilter.de/amazon-plan-of-action-poa-dos-and-donts-von-chris-mccabe/); [My Amazon Guy](https://myamazonguy.com/suspensions/submit-an-appeal-to-amazon/)), but that is about the letter being sent. **No source says a past angry message is held against a later, well-written POA.**

## Every earlier round leaves a mark that later reviewers read

No source states a published numeric cap on ordinary appeals, but the sources agree that **repeating a refused letter, even reworded, does not work and costs something**. **[P]** McCabe describes a "denial cycle" and says that if a needed document is missing, "don't appeal at all until you have it" ([ecommerceChris](https://www.ecommercechris.com/why-amazon-rejects-your-appeals-3-costly-mistakes-keeping-sellers-suspended/)). **[P]** On his podcast: "half an answer is the same as zero answer", most new clients arrive "with 3, 4, 5 denials in hand", and Leah McHugh warns that premature escalation means "you've just wasted a whole escalation path" ([ecommerceChris](https://www.ecommercechris.com/why-your-amazon-appeal-keeps-getting-denied/)). **[P]** Riverbend: "Each rejection extends the time of your account or ASIN suspension" ([Riverbend](https://riverbendconsulting.com/blog/invoices-requested-by-amazon/)). **[P]** McCabe also reports that over-escalation can leave a "FW" (Final Word) annotation on the account, after which investigators stop replying ([Webretailer, McCabe, 2024](https://www.webretailer.com/b/amazon-suspension-escalations)). That is the clearest practitioner statement that notes left by earlier rounds change how a later reviewer treats the same letter.

The commonly reported final wording is **[U]** "we have decided that you may not sell on Amazon.com. We may not respond to further emails about this issue" ([Seller Forums](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/b7e613c495112bb98c5bbdd4b9abc577)). Forum veterans split on whether "may" leaves a door open, so a tool should call this wording neither final nor open, and say so. **[P]** Riverbend reports a 2026 shift to "dispute only" appeals demanding proof the seller never broke the rule, which it describes as the end of second chances; it cites no Amazon document ([Riverbend](https://riverbendconsulting.com/blog/how-amazon-is-hurting-sellers/)).

Escalation routes are finite and run in order, and **history gates the first four**:

## A document Amazon doubted once changes what can be sent next

The sources draw a line that a tool must draw too. **"Could not be verified" or "insufficient" is a fixable gap; "forged or manipulated" is a different and much heavier finding.** **[U]** Notice wording quoted by sellers: "You have supplied documentation to Amazon which appears to be forged or manipulated. … All requested documents must be authentic and unaltered" ([Seller Forums](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/0921878a190615defa1cbe0b70b1e6d2)). In the same thread Amazon staff asked for invoices **from a new supplier**, and a community member argued that the seller's own edits and a Word-to-PDF conversion counted as manipulation. **[P]** McCabe says self-made or edited invoices are treated as forgery, investigators may annotate the account "forged invoices", and one instance can end it ([Webretailer, 2024](https://www.webretailer.com/amazon/amazon-invoice-verification/)). **[P]** Riverbend: the only permitted change is to "black out pricing information and highlight your ASIN/line item"; if an invoice is unclear, ask the supplier for a reprint ([Riverbend](https://riverbendconsulting.com/blog/invoices-requested-by-amazon/)). **[P]** One reported reactivation route is an acknowledgment form in which the seller admits the violation and agrees that a further failure means "permanent deactivation" ([Seller Union, Feb 2023](https://seller-union.com/this-is-amazons-new-approach-to-suspensions-over-manipulated-documents/)); that version is old and unconfirmed for 2026. No official source says whether a forgery finding is permanent.

Invoice checks have a consistent core. **[U]** An Amazon staff-suffix guide lists: issued within the last 365 days, quantities that match sales, product identifiers, supplier name, address and website, a completed order (no quotes or pro formas), PDFs or images rather than editable files, and pricing may be removed but nothing else ([Seller Forums, Sarah_Amzn](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/884393ff-65c3-4cd3-9664-a49286335ff7)). **[P]** Investigators phone suppliers, check that the website works and the address matches, and compare the buyer address with Seller Central; give a named, direct-dial contact and warn the supplier to expect the call ([Webretailer](https://www.webretailer.com/amazon/amazon-invoice-verification/); [ecommerceChris](https://www.ecommercechris.com/amazon-inauthentic-suspensions/)). **[P]** Two details matter for a tool: Riverbend reports a refusal on a **180-day** window, not 365, and a case where **Amazon's own units-sold figure was wrong** (131 claimed against 36 on the account), fixed by correcting it from order history ([Riverbend, 24 Sep 2026](https://riverbendconsulting.com/blog/the-art-of-being-literal-in-your-amazon-seller-account-appeal/)). The window depends on the request, so it must be read from the notice, and Amazon's figure must be recorded separately from the seller's.

## Linked accounts and held funds tie one account's fate to another's

Related accounts are the strongest single explanation for "same letter, different result". **[U, staff reply]** Amazon staff on the forum say the linked account "does share at least one significant point of information" with the other, and that the other account must be reinstated first through its own appeal, after which the seller appeals citing that reinstatement ([Seller Forums EU, Abella_Amazon](https://sellercentral-europe.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/02f329bf-fe76-458e-ac70-af671b635e06)). **[P]** Dave Bryant: "Simply being related to another account isn't resulting in suspensions. It's being related to another account that is currently suspended" ([EcomCrew](https://www.ecomcrew.com/amazon-related-accounts-suspensions/)); Amazon Sellers Lawyer disagrees and says any link can trigger it ([Amazon Sellers Lawyer](https://amazonsellerslawyer.com/blog/suspension/how-to-resolve-related-account-suspension-on-amazon/)). Either way, **the second account's appeal can be gated on the other account's status, not on its own letter.** **[V]** BSA Section 3 also lets Amazon withhold the reason where giving it "will enable you to circumvent our safeguards" ([BSA PDF](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/G/01/rainier/help./1.1_BSA_PDF_English.pdf)), which is why these notices are vague.

What links accounts is known only from practitioners. **[P]** Traverse Legal lists addresses, phones, tax information, bank accounts and cards, IPs and devices, login behaviour, shared employees and service providers, and behavioural overlaps, and says a shared bank account or tax ID "carries more weight"; it also warns "sellers should not deny facts that Amazon can prove… A false denial can make the case worse" ([Traverse Legal, Jul 2026](https://www.traverselegal.com/blog/amazon-linked-accounts/)). **[P]** Shared VAs and agencies are named triggers, and giving user permissions to someone who had a suspended account is one; the advice is to use User Permissions, never shared logins ([Amazon Sellers Lawyer](https://amazonsellerslawyer.com/related-accounts-amazon-suspension); [EcomCrew](https://www.ecomcrew.com/amazon-related-accounts-suspensions/)). **[U]** One seller reports a link through a VA abroad who never shared a device or IP ([Seller Forums](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/bb09acea-ade8-47c9-9082-d8d634b368c2)). Whether a second account needs prior approval in November 2026 is disputed among practitioners ([BellaVix](https://www.bellavix.com/can-you-have-more-than-one-amazon-seller-account-heres-what-to-know/); [MyAmazonGuy](https://myamazonguy.com/seller-central/seller-central-amazon-accounts/)), so a second account must not be treated as wrongful by default. **[P]** Opening a new account during a suspension is treated as circumvention and is "detrimental" to the original appeal ([Riverbend](https://riverbendconsulting.com/blog/suspended-amazon-account/); [McCabe on Webretailer](https://www.webretailer.com/b/amazon-seller-suspensions)).

Held funds follow the same logic. **[V]** The BSA lets Amazon withhold payments "for as long as we determine any related risks … persist", and permanently if "your account—or any other account you have operated—has been used to engage in deceptive, fraudulent, or illegal activity (including the sale of counterfeit or stolen goods)" ([BSA PDF](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/G/01/rainier/help./1.1_BSA_PDF_English.pdf)). So **another account's history can decide this account's money**. **[U]** Quoted notices say the funds investigation "is separate from a reinstatement appeal" ([Seller Forums](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/a1034002-ff58-4f01-ac2d-ef82dc73d592)). The request window is reported as 90 days in older notices and 60 days in later ones (**[U]** [UK forum summary](https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/seller-forums/discussions/t/740b3b7f-1f00-4a45-b608-4f0890475f6a); **[P]** [DAM Law Firm, Jul 2026](https://damlawfirm.com/blog/amazon-withheld-funds-appeal-guide/)), so it must be read from the seller's own notice. Identity re-verification adds a related history: **[P]** trade press reports a September 2026 video-verification sweep that hit established accounts, with some sellers refused because they did not sound like "the person running the business" ([Novadata](https://novadata.io/resources/news/amazon-section-3-video-verification-sweep-september-2026)); Amazon published nothing. That overlaps with the people link: if a VA really runs the business, both questions surface.

## Changes made before appealing are evidence only when dated and disclosed

**[P]** Deleting listings does not clear anything: "the mark against their selling record doesn't go anywhere", and Amazon wants evidence such as removal order numbers, how products to stop were chosen, whether the supplier relationship was ended, and how new suppliers are tested ([ecommerceChris](https://www.ecommercechris.com/amazon-inauthentic-suspensions/)). **[P]** Relisting the same product under a new ASIN is treated as evasion, and Account Health reps have sometimes wrongly suggested it ([Seller Performance Solutions podcast](https://podcasts.bcast.fm/e/p8m69zjn-relisting-is-not-an-option)). No source settles whether a deletion must be disclosed, or what editing a listing during an open review does. The practical rule is simple: real, dated, evidenced changes belong in the corrective actions; a deletion offered as the whole fix should draw a warning; relisting or a new account during review should draw a strong one.

## Why the same violation and letter can end differently

No Amazon source explains outcome variance. The sourced explanations fall into two groups. **Inputs the seller controls:** the quality and consistency of earlier submissions (rushed or template letters push a case into a "generic-denial lane" **[P]**, [podcast summary](https://rss.com/podcasts/seller-performance-solutions/2399244)), annotations provoked by over-escalation **[P]**, a document once called manipulated, an undisclosed or still-deactivated related account, a seller's own units-sold correction, and whether every point in Amazon's last reply was answered. **Inputs the seller does not control:** which investigator picks it up (McCabe says investigators "sometimes miss important details" **[P]**, [Webretailer](https://www.webretailer.com/b/amazon-suspension-escalations)), which team handles the violation type, AHA eligibility, and teams contradicting each other (**[P]** Riverbend's case of a Seller Challenge team approving a supply chain that another team kept denying, [Riverbend](https://riverbendconsulting.com/blog/the-art-of-being-literal-in-your-amazon-seller-account-appeal/)). "Different reviewer, different answer" is widely assumed but undocumented. Whether reviewers weigh prior violations when judging a POA is plausible and also undocumented. A tool can honestly help only with the first group, and should say so.

## AppealDeck today vs the findings

"Today" is the verified description given for this report, spot-checked in `src/` (read only). ## What to build, in order

**First: the account background card and the per-document history.** These close the two largest documented risks. Add one optional "About this account" card beside "Earlier attempts" with five plain questions: earlier warnings for this same problem in the last 180 days (count, dates, answered or not); any other seller account owned, run or formerly run; who has or had access and how; a new account or relisting during review; what was changed before appealing, with dates. Each "yes" adds a plain line to "Before you send", and the letter uses only what the seller typed. Alongside it, ask for each document "Has Amazon seen this before, and what did it say?" and block reuse of anything Amazon called forged or manipulated. The reason: repeats are the one history fact Amazon's own policy names, a related or undisclosed account can block an otherwise good letter, and a document already flagged as manipulated is the costliest thing a seller can resend.

**Second: the checks that make documents survive verification.** Read the invoice window length from the notice (180 or 365), add Amazon's units-sold figure beside the seller's, add the supplier-reachability checklist, and show the edit/convert warning on every upload. The reason: practitioners report refusals on exactly these points, and none of them can be fixed by better wording.

**Third: rounds and routes.** Record escalation routes already used, warn before an escalation when no strong letter is on record, detect "previously alerted" and "repeat violations" wording in the decoder, add the acknowledgment-tick note, and build the dated timeline from data the case already holds. The reason: escalations are finite and history-gated, and a timeline is the one account-level item a firm explicitly asks for.

**Later, on a trigger:** branching the related-account records list by "mine / former / family / stranger" once the first related-account cases come in; the verification-history questions; the sales-velocity question when a notice cites sales history; extra tone rules in the critic.

**Do not build:** any score, chance, risk level or prediction from history (D6, and no source supports one); an AHR input that changes advice; questions about account age, lifetime sales or the tone of old messages as appeal factors; any sentence in product copy that claims to be Amazon's wording until a signed-in seller has checked it against the live help page; and a fixed list of "Amazon's linking signals", since Amazon publishes none.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. "How much does history count?" has no sourced answer and should never get a number. "Which facts in the account's past change what the next letter must say, attach, or avoid?" has a clear one: same-policy repeats, earlier rounds and routes used, what Amazon said about each document, other accounts and the people with access, and dated changes. Those facts explain most of the founder's "same POA, different result" without any appeal to luck, and each one maps to a question and a check rather than to a prediction.

The second shift is that much of what decides the outcome sits outside the letter entirely: a related account's status, a document's past, Amazon's own unit count, a supplier who does not answer the phone. A tool that only writes better letters cannot reach those. A tool that asks the right history questions before drafting, and refuses to let a flagged document or a near-copy go out, can, and that is the part a seller cannot get from a chatbot.
