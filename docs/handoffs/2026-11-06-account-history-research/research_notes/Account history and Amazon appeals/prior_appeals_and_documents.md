# Prior appeals and prior documents: how Amazon treats a seller who appeals again

Research date: 6 November 2026. Tags: [V] verified official Amazon source (URL); [P] practitioner experience (name + URL); [U] unverified, forum anecdote or seller-quoted Amazon wording.

**Method caveat (read first):** Seller Central help pages (for example the Seller Code of Conduct, G1801) are rendered by JavaScript and returned no text when fetched (https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G1801). No Amazon help-page text could be checked directly. The nearest thing to official wording available here is (a) posts on Amazon's Seller Forums by accounts with an Amazon staff suffix (`_Amazon`, `_Amzn`), and (b) Amazon notices that sellers quote on the forums. Both are marked [U] unless the staff identity is clear, and even then they are forum posts, not policy pages. **There are no [V] claims in these notes.** Before any of this becomes product copy, the help pages should be read while signed in to Seller Central.

No success rate is given as a fact below. Where a source states a number, it is quoted and tagged.

## 1. Is there a limit on the number of appeals? What happens after resubmitting the same POA or after several rejections?

### Takeaway
No source found states a published numeric cap on appeals. Practitioners and forum veterans agree that resubmitting the same or a lightly reworded POA does not work and can make things worse (a "denial cycle"). After repeated failures Amazon sends boilerplate replies and, eventually, a "we may not respond to further emails" message. Several sources report a numeric limit of three Seller Challenges per 180 days, but that applies to the separate Account Health Assurance (AHA) Seller Challenge benefit, not to ordinary appeals.

### Cited Findings
- [U] A forum veteran (not staff), about 8 years ago: "The amount of appeals which a seller can submit is not unlimited." No number is given. In the same thread, the seller quotes Seller Performance emails from Dec 2018: "We may not reply to further emails about this issue." — [Seller Forums, "How many plans of action can i submit?"](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/7ff370c2b296308fbce4358f5830bfe2)
- [P] Chris McCabe (ecommerceChris, former Amazon investigator) describes a "denial cycle" in which repeated denials make later reviewers more likely to deny again. He warns against "resubmitting over and over to no avail", and says that if a needed document is missing, "don't appeal at all until you have it." The page is undated. — [ecommerceChris, "Why Amazon Rejects Your Appeals: 3 Costly Mistakes"](https://www.ecommercechris.com/why-amazon-rejects-your-appeals-3-costly-mistakes-keeping-sellers-suspended/)
- [P] McCabe: "If it's not working, if you're clearly stuck, don't go from nine appeals submitted to 19," and "most people are coming to us with 3, 4, 5 denials in hand." Leah McHugh: "the quickest way to resolve a case is to deny a reinstatement," and premature escalation means "you've just wasted a whole escalation path." McCabe: "half an answer is the same as zero answer." The podcast episode (S1 E197) is undated. — [ecommerceChris, "Why Your Amazon Appeal Keeps Getting Denied"](https://www.ecommercechris.com/why-your-amazon-appeal-keeps-getting-denied/)
- [P] The same episode warns against Account Health checklists whose boxes acknowledge a violation. Sellers are "admitting guilt while doing so" (McHugh). — [ecommerceChris](https://www.ecommercechris.com/why-your-amazon-appeal-keeps-getting-denied/)
- [U] Forum threads report the same boilerplate rejection on every attempt ("not enough information"). One seller appealed more than 12 times with detailed plans and documents and got the same generic response each time. — [Seller Forums, "My Appeals keep on getting rejected"](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/7ee9fa30-c738-490a-bf3c-afbb02e9ec2b)
- [U] Forum anecdote: after too many appeals Amazon stops answering, and the seller must wait "around 30 days" before submitting again. Unverified; no Amazon source was found. — [Seller Forums, "Amazon Appeal Rejected in Less Than a Minute"](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/76b51c0a-d7c3-4175-a3c7-83dd0558e2bb)
- [U, vendor blog] The sources disagree on this point. One blog says Amazon does not limit the number of appeals. Another claims that "after three or four identical or weak submissions, Amazon's automation assumes you have no new information … and terminates the thread." No Amazon source supports either claim. — [appeals24x7](https://appeals24x7.com/amazon-appeal-rejection-repeating-appeals-fails/); summarised in search results with [Mr Jeff](https://www.mrjeffamz.com/blog/what-to-do-when-amazon-rejects-your-appeal-next-steps) (that URL now returns 404)
- [U, vendor blog] "Each appeal you submit is permanently attached to your case," so a rushed or incomplete appeal can hurt later appeals. — reported in search results for [adnabu](https://blog.adnabu.com/amazon/amazon-seller-account-suspended/) (not fetched)
- [P] Riverbend Consulting (Kayleigh Cockerill, updated 9 Jun 2025): "Each rejection extends the time of your account or ASIN suspension." The article recommends outside help if "the same vague response" keeps coming back. — [Riverbend, "How to Navigate an Amazon Invoice Request"](https://riverbendconsulting.com/blog/invoices-requested-by-amazon/)
- [P/U] Seller Challenge (an AHA benefit, reported to have started 29 Sep 2025) is described as an enhanced review after the standard appeal fails. It is limited to "three opportunities … every 180 days", and sellers who lose AHA eligibility "will no longer have access to your remaining Seller Challenges" (ppc.land cites Amazon's FAQ). — reported by [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/amazon-introduces-seller-challenge-feature-for-enforcement-appeals/) and [My Amazon Guy](https://myamazonguy.com/news/amazon-seller-challenge-benefit/) (both from search results; not fetched or confirmed against Amazon's FAQ)
- [P] Riverbend's 24 Sep 2026 case study describes a Seller Challenge team approving a supply chain while a different Amazon team (brand approval) kept denying it within minutes. Teams can contradict each other. — [Riverbend, "The Art of Being Literal"](https://riverbendconsulting.com/blog/the-art-of-being-literal-in-your-amazon-seller-account-appeal/)

### Inferences
- A tool should record every prior attempt (date, channel, what was sent, Amazon's exact reply) and treat "same text, no new evidence" as the main thing to warn against.
- Ordinary appeals have no published numeric limit to show sellers. The Seller Challenge "3 per 180 days" figure is reported, unconfirmed, and specific to that benefit. It is worth recording whether the seller has used Seller Challenges, but only with an "as reported" caveat.
- Escalation paths (Seller Challenge, executive escalation) appear to be finite and to "use up" a chance. A tool should ask which ones the seller has already used.

### Gaps
- Amazon's own wording on resubmitting the same POA was not found. The help pages could not be fetched.
- Whether Amazon systematically auto-replies to repeat submissions ("bot replies") is asserted only by vendors. No Amazon statement was found.

## 2. What does a final rejection look like, and is anything possible after it?

### Takeaway
The commonly reported final wording is "we have decided that you may not sell on Amazon.com" plus "We may not respond to further emails about this issue." Forum veterans treat it as near-final but note that it says *may*. The options seen after it are executive escalation, a different and genuinely new appeal, a funds-release request after 90 days, and AAA arbitration under the Business Solutions Agreement (BSA), which is outside what a POA tool does. Riverbend reports a 2026 shift to "dispute only" workflows in which a seller who did break the rule cannot use the appeal path at all.

### Cited Findings
- [U] Amazon wording quoted by a seller, about 9 years ago: "We reviewed your account and the information you provided, and we have decided that you may not sell on Amazon.com. We may not respond to further emails about this issue." Replies from other sellers range from "effectively over; executive office is the only option" to "'may' implies Amazon might still reply". Another reply says a message from Performance leaves one chance, while a message from the executive office leaves none. — [Seller Forums, "We may not respond to further emails about this issue"](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/b7e613c495112bb98c5bbdd4b9abc577); see also [the companion thread](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/710c2275d7c3ef9dfddbc2d5ca5e752f)
- [U] Forum veteran: after the final message, sellers "can still try" emailing executive escalations, but "the chances of a successful reinstatement at that point are rather low." — [Seller Forums](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/7ff370c2b296308fbce4358f5830bfe2)
- [U] Amazon notice quoted in the same thread: "If you do not appeal this decision in 90 days, we will permanently withhold any payments to you." — [Seller Forums](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/7ff370c2b296308fbce4358f5830bfe2)
- [U] An Amazon moderator is reported as saying that after 90 days of enforcement a seller may appeal for funds by emailing disbursement-appeals@amazon.com. Amazon staff are also reported as saying that future funds requests "may be treated as your final appeal". — reported in search results for the [Seller Forums funds threads](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/c65b73a88af95b460947e65d87fea193) (not fetched)
- [P] Riverbend (updated 23 Sep 2026) says Seller Performance increasingly allows only a "dispute only" appeal that demands "proof that the seller never broke the rules". "Since the seller did break the rules, they will never be reinstated" … "Your ASIN is dead, or your account is blocked. Forever." This contrasts with a past "24-year track record of second chances". The claim is the consultancy's own report and cites no Amazon document. — [Riverbend, "No Second Chances?!"](https://riverbendconsulting.com/blog/how-amazon-is-hurting-sellers/)
- [P] McCabe: after a deactivation, "they will reinstate you only if you disprove the accuracy of that action" (undated). — [ecommerceChris](https://www.ecommercechris.com/why-amazon-rejects-your-appeals-3-costly-mistakes-keeping-sellers-suspended/)
- [P, law-firm advocacy] Arbitration under the BSA's binding clause is described as the seller's remaining route. Amazon's position is reported to be that the agreement is at-will, so it need not reinstate even when wrong. Hard 2 Find Accessories v. Amazon (W.D. Wash. 2014, aff'd 9th Cir. 2017) is cited. — [amazonsellerslawyer.com, seller disputes](https://amazonsellerslawyer.com/amazon-seller-disputes); [Revision Legal](https://revisionlegal.com/internet-law/can-i-sue-amazon-amazons-forced-arbitration-clause/) (search-result summaries, not fetched)

### Inferences
- A tool should ask whether the seller has received wording like "may not sell on Amazon" or "may not respond to further emails", and whether it came from Seller Performance or executive escalations. If it has, the honest message is that the ordinary appeal path is probably exhausted. The tool should mention funds-release timing, and that arbitration is a legal matter outside its scope (consistent with AppealDeck's existing "Where this service stops" Terms section).
- Wording that says *may* must not be presented as either final or open. Sources disagree, and the tool should say so plainly.

### Gaps
- The current (2026) final-rejection template wording was not found. Only old forum quotes were available.
- No official description of "dispute only" appeals was found. The only source is Riverbend.

## 3. Documents Amazon previously rejected or found falsified: what happens, is the finding permanent, and what should be sent instead?

### Takeaway
"Could not be verified" or "insufficient" is a different and recoverable category from "appears to be forged or manipulated". A forgery finding is treated as a Seller Code of Conduct breach (usually cited as BSA Section 3). Reported reactivation is by an acknowledgment form that includes the seller agreeing that a further failure means *permanent* deactivation, plus invoices from a new supplier. Practitioners say a forgery annotation stays on the account and that one instance can end it. The only edits ever allowed are blacking out prices and highlighting the ASIN.

### Cited Findings
- [U] Amazon notice wording quoted by sellers: "You have supplied documentation to Amazon which appears to be forged or manipulated." "All requested documents must be authentic and unaltered." The reactivation options are an explanation or evidence that the documents are not forged. — [Seller Forums, "Account Suspension - Section 3 Forged/Manipulated Invoices"](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/0921878a190615defa1cbe0b70b1e6d2)
- [U] The same thread reports that Amazon asked for final invoices or receipts **from a new supplier**, showing at least 10 units, with the supplier's name, phone, address and website; pricing may be removed. Staff (Wade_Amazon) said invoices must reflect the past 365 days of sales, that Amazon may contact the supplier, and that the seller should highlight the ASIN. If the products or supplier were inauthentic, the seller should submit a plan of action. Staff (Antonio_Amazon) described two paths: send valid invoices, or follow the reactivation steps. Outcome: the seller's three new-supplier invoices were rejected ("You have not sent us sufficient information that we previously requested"); about 8 or 9 later appeals failed; no reactivation by the last post. — [Seller Forums](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/0921878a190615defa1cbe0b70b1e6d2)
- [U] A community member in the same thread argued that the seller's own edits and a Word-to-PDF conversion counted as manipulation. The point is that re-typing or "cleaning up" a document can itself be a finding. — [Seller Forums](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/0921878a190615defa1cbe0b70b1e6d2)
- [P] Acknowledgment form wording (as screenshotted): "I acknowledge that my Account violated Amazon's policies and I wish to reactivate my Account", "I Acknowledge that submitting forged or manipulated documents …", agreement to the Seller Code of Conduct, and "failure to do so will result in the permanent deactivation of my account". Accepted files are PDF, PNG or GIF and "must be authentic and unaltered". FBA inventory for the flagged items is ineligible for removal. Per the article, this form replaces a detailed POA. Kika Angelic, 25 Feb 2023 (older policy version; the author sells appeal services). — [Seller Union, "Amazon's new approach to suspensions over Manipulated Documents"](https://seller-union.com/this-is-amazons-new-approach-to-suspensions-over-manipulated-documents/)
- [U] An Amazon forum moderator reply is reported as saying that if the seller claims a technical error, "you would need to provide evidence for this alongside a document that can meet the original requirements", and that Amazon "may validate any information that you provide with third party services or government agencies". — search-result summary of [Seller Forums (EU)](https://sellercentral-europe.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/34c459b5-e5aa-4463-9a99-3238f22cc7e4) (not fetched; the thread attribution is approximate)
- [U] Forum reply: "Deactivations for section 3 (code of conduct) paired with a dropshipping or counterfeit policy violations are not being considered for reactivation. Having the counterfeit claim withdrawn is your best bet." — search-result summary of Seller Forums threads (exact thread not isolated)
- [P] McCabe (via Webretailer, updated 26 Feb 2024): self-made or edited invoices are treated as forgery. Investigators may annotate the account "forged invoices", and getting caught once can end the account. Handwritten invoices are not acceptable. — [Webretailer, "7 Steps to Pass Amazon's New Invoice Verification Process"](https://www.webretailer.com/amazon/amazon-invoice-verification/)
- [P] Riverbend (9 Jun 2025): the only permitted alteration is that "Amazon will allow you to black out pricing information and highlight your ASIN/line item." Altering an invoice is "one of the most critical mistakes you can make". If an invoice is unclear, ask the supplier for a reprint rather than editing it. — [Riverbend](https://riverbendconsulting.com/blog/invoices-requested-by-amazon/)
- [P] ecommerceChris: if an invoice lacks an invoice number, quantity, SKU or description, return it to the supplier for "an updated, completed invoice". An unverifiable supplier leads Amazon to "annotate the account that your supplier can't be verified". — [ecommerceChris, "10 things … Inauthentic Account Suspensions"](https://www.ecommercechris.com/amazon-inauthentic-suspensions/)
- [U, vendor estimate] Helium10 estimates "fewer than 10% of all Code of Conduct suspensions get reinstated". This is a vendor figure with no methodology; it is reported here only because a source states it. — [Helium10](https://www.helium10.com/?p=10262) (search-result summary)

### Inferences
- A tool must ask, for every document the seller plans to send, whether Amazon has seen it before and what Amazon said about it. "Could not be verified" or "insufficient" means fix the gap: a fuller invoice, a reachable supplier contact, correct dates and units. "Forged or manipulated" means never resend that document or anything derived from it. The next response must come from different, untouched records (practitioners and the quoted notice say a new supplier), and the letter must not argue that the earlier document was fine unless the seller has independent proof.
- The warning text should state plainly that editing, re-typing, converting a Word document to PDF, or "fixing" a supplier's invoice can itself be treated as manipulation. Only blacking out prices and highlighting the ASIN are reported as allowed.
- The acknowledgment-form route asks the seller to admit a violation. McHugh's warning about ticking acknowledgment boxes applies, so the tool should point out what a tick admits rather than advise either way.

### Gaps
- No official statement was found on whether a forgery finding is permanent. The only sources are the "permanent deactivation" wording on the acknowledgment form (about repeat failure) and practitioner claims.
- The current (2026) form of the forged-document reactivation flow was not confirmed. The Seller Union description is from Feb 2023.

## 4. How Amazon verifies invoices

### Takeaway
Staff forum posts and seller-quoted notices give the same core rules. Invoices must be issued within 365 days, show a completed transaction (no pro forma or quotes), have quantities that match sales, carry product identifiers, and include full supplier contact details (name, address, phone, website). Amazon may contact the supplier. Practitioners add that investigators phone suppliers, check supplier websites and addresses, compare the buyer address with Seller Central, and look for signs of editing. Some requests use a 180-day window instead.

### Cited Findings
- [U, staff-suffix post] Sarah_Amzn's guide (about 2024): "Issued within the last 365 days"; "Quantities match sales volumes"; "Product identifiers (name, model, barcode etc.)"; "Supplier contact details (name, address, website)"; "They need to show a completed, fulfilled order (no quotes/proformas)"; "Invoices must be authentic PDFs or images, not editable formats"; Amazon "may reject invoices that appear inauthentic, altered or lacking required info"; "Pricing can be removed, but all other details must be visible"; "Suppliers should have a verifiable online presence." — [Seller Forums, "A Guide to Amazon's Invoice Requirements"](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/884393ff-65c3-4cd3-9664-a49286335ff7)
- [U] Seller-quoted notice: invoices issued in the last 365 days for the listed ASINs, with supplier name, phone, address and website; Amazon may contact the supplier to verify, and keeps the contact details confidential. Other requests ask for sales volume across all marketplaces over 365 days and the full supply chain, including manufacturer contact details. — search-result summaries of [Seller Forums threads](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/208f868649cb58cc19ebfa5b555fd8d9)
- [U] Amazon sometimes asks for the supplier's own supplier invoices (upstream). — [Seller Forums, "Amazon asked supplier's supplier issued in the last 365 days"](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/81a2468e-e41a-4cb4-b97e-7b708c055403)
- [P] McCabe via Webretailer (26 Feb 2024): investigators "make phone calls, send emails, check websites and more". If they hit a roadblock reaching the supplier "they might give up", so give a direct-dial number and a named contact. They "really want the URL there"; an under-construction or generic site is a problem. Supplier addresses must match what investigators find online, or the invoice may be called "unverifiable". The buyer address must exactly match Seller Central. Invoices older than a year are "not acceptable documentation". Invoiced quantities must match or exceed sales; if sales outstrip invoiced units Amazon rejects the invoices and may bar the ASIN. Keep at least 90 days of sales covered. Signs of editing include blurry logos, mismatched colours, misaligned lines and re-edited numbers. — [Webretailer](https://www.webretailer.com/amazon/amazon-invoice-verification/)
- [P] ecommerceChris: calling suppliers is part of the procedure, but investigators "appear to skip this step sometimes". Avoid "an 800 number", and prepare the supplier for the call. — [ecommerceChris](https://www.ecommercechris.com/amazon-inauthentic-suspensions/)
- [P] Riverbend (9 Jun 2025): the company name must match the Seller Central legal entity. Commercial invoices, bills of lading and packing slips do not replace an invoice. Pro forma invoices are unlikely to be accepted, so get an invoice showing a completed, zero-balance transaction. Long-form and retail-arbitrage receipts are disfavoured. "Amazon often contacts suppliers to verify the authenticity of your sourcing." An ASIN may not be reinstated if there is "a significant discrepancy" between purchases and sales. — [Riverbend](https://riverbendconsulting.com/blog/invoices-requested-by-amazon/)
- [P] Riverbend case study (24 Sep 2026): the invoice was rejected because the only invoice listing the item fell outside a **180-day** window, and because Amazon claimed 131 units sold when the account showed 36. The fix was to correct Amazon's unit count from order history and identify each document precisely. — [Riverbend](https://riverbendconsulting.com/blog/the-art-of-being-literal-in-your-amazon-seller-account-appeal/)
- **Disagreement:** some sources say 365 days, and some say "365 days, or delivery/purchase within the last 180 days"; Riverbend's case was refused on 180 days. The window appears to depend on the specific request. — [search-result summary citing appealsdoctor and e-cabilly](https://www.appealsdoctor.com/blog/amazon-invoice-verification-how-to-prove-authenticity-without-issues-in-2026); [Riverbend](https://riverbendconsulting.com/blog/the-art-of-being-literal-in-your-amazon-seller-account-appeal/)

### Inferences
- A tool should take the window from the seller's actual notice (365 or 180 days) rather than hard-coding one. AppealDeck's existing invoice check uses 365 days and units sold (CLAUDE.md, 10 Oct 2026), so it should also accept a 180-day window when the notice states one.
- A tool should ask whether the supplier has a reachable named contact, a working website and an address that matches, and whether the seller has told the supplier to expect a call. These are reported failure points that no wording can fix.
- A tool should record Amazon's stated units-sold figure separately from the seller's own figure, because Amazon's figure can be wrong (Riverbend's case).

### Gaps
- The official supply-chain documentation help page could not be fetched.
- How Amazon handles verification calls the supplier does not answer is described only anecdotally.

## 5. Changes made before appealing: deleting listings or ASINs, changing supplier, closing listings, editing during review

### Takeaway
No source says deleting listings resolves anything. The record stays, and creating a new ASIN or relisting the same product is treated as evasion. Removing inventory, terminating a problem supplier and changing sourcing are actions practitioners expect to be **disclosed with evidence** (removal order numbers, termination, a new vetting process), dated, in the corrective-actions section. No official rule on editing listings during review was found.

### Cited Findings
- [P] ecommerceChris: sellers may handle one or two complaints by removing FBA inventory and deleting listings, "but the mark against their selling record doesn't go anywhere". — search-result summary of [ecommerceChris inauthentic suspensions](https://www.ecommercechris.com/amazon-inauthentic-suspensions/)
- [P] ecommerceChris: Amazon expects evidence of what was done, for example "Did you pull inventory out of FBA to reexamine the items for defects" and "what are the removal order numbers?". It wants an explanation of how the seller decided which products to stop selling, whether the seller ended a problem supplier ("did you terminate the business relationship?"), and whether new suppliers are test-bought before listing. — [ecommerceChris](https://www.ecommercechris.com/amazon-inauthentic-suspensions/)
- [P] ecommerceChris: in an evasive-behaviour case, the dispute should prove "the immediate deletion of duplicate ASINs" and show controls against listing circumvention. Separately, "making a new listing is not an option, because Amazon sees that as evasive behavior". — search-result summaries of [ecommerceChris, "Evasive Behavior Suspensions"](https://www.ecommercechris.com/evasive-behavior-suspensions-duplicate-asins-and-tricks-go-badly/) and [ASIN appeal page](https://www.ecommercechris.com/amazon-seller-services/amazon-asin-appeal/)
- [P] Seller Performance Solutions podcast ("Relisting is not an option"): creating a new ASIN for the same product is against policy unless the product is materially different. Relisted items get suspended again because the original suspension was never resolved. Account Health and Seller Support reps have sometimes suggested relisting, so that advice is unreliable. — [podcast episode](https://podcasts.bcast.fm/e/p8m69zjn-relisting-is-not-an-option) (search-result summary)
- [U] An Amazon forum how-to says not to create new accounts while an appeal is under review, because it can lead to permanent suspension. — search-result summary of [Seller Forums, "How To: Appeal Account Suspension"](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/c50b687d-e4b4-4c9a-830e-03cff607bc38)
- [U] In the forged-document flow, FBA inventory for flagged items is reported as ineligible for removal. — [Seller Union, 2023](https://seller-union.com/this-is-amazons-new-approach-to-suspensions-over-manipulated-documents/)
- [P, vendor] Guides advise listing completed actions with dates. — [velocitysellers (Oct 2026)](https://www.velocitysellers.com/2026/10/15/amazon-plan-of-action-template/) (search-result summary)

### Inferences
- A tool should ask what the seller has already changed (listings deleted or closed, inventory removed with removal order IDs, supplier dropped, new supplier and how it was vetted, listing edits) and when. Real, dated, evidenced changes belong in the corrective actions. A deletion offered as the whole fix should trigger a warning.
- A tool should warn hard against relisting the same product under a new ASIN or opening another account during review.
- Whether a deletion "must" be disclosed is not settled by any official source. Practitioners treat undisclosed changes as a missed chance to show corrective action, not as a violation in themselves.

### Gaps
- No official or practitioner source was found on editing a listing *during* an open review: whether it resets the review, helps or hurts.
- No official Amazon text says deleted ASINs must be disclosed in the POA.

## 6. What must change between a rejected appeal and the next one

### Takeaway
Practitioners agree on the main points. Read the whole of Amazon's reply and answer every point it raises. Add new evidence rather than new wording. Correct factual errors in Amazon's own message, including its numbers. Never send a template or obviously AI-written text. Do not send a POA when Amazon did not ask for one. Do not send unrelated documents. Do not escalate early. If a required document is missing, wait until you have it.

### Cited Findings
- [P] McCabe and McHugh: answer everything Amazon asked ("half an answer is the same as zero answer"); sellers often "failed to read the message from them". Do not send "unrelated documentation that has nothing to do with what they have asked you for". Customise every submission ("There is no point sending a template"). Avoid AI-written text ("they just don't take you seriously"). Build a track record before escalating. — [ecommerceChris](https://www.ecommercechris.com/why-your-amazon-appeal-keeps-getting-denied/)
- [P] McCabe: "Don't submit a Plan of Action, if they didn't tell you it's required to reinstate!" New strategies "must reference already executed steps that you tested for success". Have the appeal reviewed "before you revise and resubmit". — [ecommerceChris](https://www.ecommercechris.com/why-amazon-rejects-your-appeals-3-costly-mistakes-keeping-sellers-suspended/)
- [P] Riverbend (24 Sep 2026): be literal. Correct Amazon's wrong figures (131 → 36 units). Name each document precisely (payment records versus "order confirmations"). Cite any earlier Amazon decision that accepted the same supply chain. Ask Amazon to name the specific document, quantity or data point it still finds insufficient. — [Riverbend](https://riverbendconsulting.com/blog/the-art-of-being-literal-in-your-amazon-seller-account-appeal/)
- [U] A forum reply says a POA sent when documents such as invoices are required will be rejected; if documents are rejected, the seller needs additional or supporting evidence that validates them. — search-result summary of [Seller Forums](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/76b51c0a-d7c3-4175-a3c7-83dd0558e2bb)

### Inferences
For a tool such as AppealDeck, the next letter should be built from the difference between rounds, not rewritten from scratch:
- **What the tool should ask:** Amazon's last reply in full; every prior submission's text and attachments; which escalation channels have been used; any final-sounding wording received; for each document, whether Amazon saw it and what it said; and what changed in the business since the last attempt.
- **Warnings worth showing:**
  - no new fact or evidence since the last attempt (AppealDeck's change report already does this);
  - an attachment that Amazon flagged as forged or manipulated;
  - an edited or re-typed document;
  - an invoice outside the window stated in the notice, or units that do not cover sales;
  - a supplier with no reachable contact or website;
  - a POA when Amazon asked only for documents;
  - attachments unrelated to the request;
  - relisting or a new account during review;
  - an acknowledgment tick that admits a violation;
  - an escalation channel about to be used before a strong appeal is on record.
- None of these warnings should promise an outcome.

### Gaps
- None of the practitioner advice is backed by official Amazon guidance; all of it is experience-based and largely from firms that sell appeal services.
- No source quantifies whether new evidence changes outcomes, and none should be invented.
