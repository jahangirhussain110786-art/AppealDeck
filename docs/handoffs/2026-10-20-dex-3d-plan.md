# Dex in real 3D: what I can do, and what I should not fake

## The honest answer

**What I can do well:** put the exact mascot picture on every page, make it turn toward the pointer, bob, hop and swap moods. That is live now (see "What is live").

**What I cannot do to your standard:** model, sculpt and rig a production 3D character from code. I can build a simple procedural 3D shape in three.js (a rounded box, a clip, a cape, balls for gloves), but it would look like a toy next to the artwork you chose, and you have already told me that hand-built hands and shoes are not good enough. I will not ship that.

**What would work:** get a real 3D model file (a `.glb`) of your exact design, then I build everything around it. The model must come from one of:

1. **An image-to-3D tool**, fed with your approved pictures (Meshy, Tripo, Rodin and similar). It gives a textured `.glb` in a few minutes. Quality varies, so you pick the best of several tries.
2. **A 3D artist**, from your pictures and the pose sheet. A fixed price job, usually the best result. Ask for a rigged model (a skeleton) if you want arms that wave, and a plain static model if you only want him to turn and bob.

## What a plain, static model already allows

No rig needed. With one `.glb` I can do:

- turn left, right, up and down to follow the pointer or a tap (rotating the whole model),
- lean, bob, squash and stretch, hop and spin,
- change a "mood" by tilting, bouncing and swapping small parts if they are separate meshes,
- a soft real-time shadow and light that moves with him.

A **rigged** model adds waving, pointing, thumbs-up, blinking and eye movement.

## What to ask for (paste this to the tool or the artist)

> A cute 3D mascot character, a living paper document, stylised soft 2.5D look, matching the attached pictures exactly. Rounded white paper body with page edges, a navy binder clip on top, an orange folded corner, an orange check mark and grey text lines on the front, a navy cape with an orange hem, two navy arms with white cartoon gloves (thumb and four fingers), navy legs with orange bands, chunky navy boots with ivory soles.
> Deliver as .glb, under 3 MB, PBR textures at 2048 px at most, a clean mesh under 60,000 triangles, a neutral standing pose with arms slightly away from the body, the face as a separate texture or separate eye meshes if possible.
> Optional: a skeleton with arm, head-tilt and eye controls.

Attach: the chosen hero picture and the five mood pictures (`docs/handoffs/assets/poses/`).

## What I will build when I have the file

- a lazy-loaded 3D guide in the same corner of every page (it loads only after the page is usable, and falls back to today's picture on slow phones or if WebGL is off),
- turning toward the pointer, idle sway, hop, and a mood change per page,
- a budget: no more than about 3 MB loaded, no effect on page speed scores, and still for people who want less motion,
- the same safety rules as today: never cheerful where a seller may be reading bad news, one short line, never a chat box.

## What is live now

The picture version on every page: the exact design, a small 3D-style tilt toward the pointer, bobbing, a hop every so often, a one-line hint, and a "Hide Dex" choice that is remembered. When the 3D model arrives, it replaces the picture in the same place.
