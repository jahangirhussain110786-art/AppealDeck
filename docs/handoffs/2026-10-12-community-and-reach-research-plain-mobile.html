A forum, free tools and support: what the research says

You asked three things: should we build a place where sellers talk and get answers from us or from experts; what free, open building blocks could make the tool feel complete; and what gives every seller a reason to look once. Four research agents checked the web and our own code. This page is the plain reading. The full file with every source is 2026-10-12-community-and-reach-research.md.

The short answer

No forum. No experts. Yes to thirteen free, boring things that make the product feel supported. Then show up where sellers already talk.

13things to do now, all free
19things to wait on
24things to skip

A forum of our own: not now. Sellers already have big, busy places to talk. The one independent forum in this niche has about 116 active users a month after years. An empty forum next to a product with zero cases looks like a dead product. The free substitute: you answer suspension threads in the existing groups, two hours a week.

"Experts answer": no, in any form. Three rules block it: our own D5 (no expert without a signed agreement and a paid audition), the US regulator's DoNotPay order (an "expert" claim needs real, named experts), and the payment company's banned list, which names "access to a community of experts" in so many words. Paddle is our only way to take money; a visible human service risks the whole site.

Free building blocks: yes. Thirteen items, all free, about 10 to 15 hours of work in total. None adds a feature. They make the product feel looked after.

Two things you should know. Our code on GitHub is public (checked today: anyone can read it). And the visitor counter is switched off until one setting is filled in, so nothing can be measured yet.

Should we build a discussion forum?

Not now. Here is why, with the numbers.

  • The places already exist, and they are bigr/AmazonSeller 99,000 · r/FulfillmentByAmazon 146,000 · ASGTG about 71,000 to 77,000 · Friendly Advice Worldwide 52,000 · Extreme Commerce and Enablers about 1.2 million each · Amazon's own forum with Amazon staff. A new forum would compete with these for the same few hundred people who answer suspension posts.
  • Independent forums in this niche stay smallsellersasksellers.com: 116 active users and 5 sign-ups in the last 30 days, after years. The other one carries casino spam. No vendor we checked runs its own forum; they all use Facebook groups.
  • A forum needs about 100 people posting a month to look aliveWhich usually means 1,000 to 10,000 members. Discourse itself says launching empty is "the worst thing you can do" and it takes "months if not years". We have zero cases and 2 to 3 hours a week.
  • Hosting what people post brings real dutiesPakistan's PECA 2025 (complaint handling, fines), a US copyright agent, UK defamation rules, EU privacy law if Europeans post. And the first posts would be the ones we must keep out: fake-invoice sources and "insider contact" offers (Bloomberg, June 2026).
What gives the same feeling, for free You answer 3 to 5 suspension threads a week in the existing groups with a plain reading of the poster's own notice (X-01). Later, a "copy for the forum" button that strips names and IDs so a seller can post their result without breaking any rule. And a free hosted Discourse stays on hold behind a written trigger (X-14): 50 cases started and the same question arriving from 10 different people, or 5 active writers asking for a shared place.

Should we, or experts, answer sellers' cases?

No. Not us, not "experts", not an AI.

  • Humans called expertsD5 first: a signed agreement and a paid test on made-up cases before anyone touches anything. Then the US rules: an expert must really be one, and must really have done the review. The only honest wording, ever, is a fact: "Reviewed by [full name], who has written Amazon appeals since [year]." Never "expert-verified", never a badge.
  • The payment companyPaddle's banned list: "pure consulting or advisory services, including legal advice, coaching... and access to a community of experts". Paddle also "reserves the right to exercise judgment". If you ever want a human review, ask Paddle in writing first, and keep the answer. The step-by-step path is in the full file (section 7).
  • An AI answering in publicOur AI key is on the free tier (20 requests a day). A free-text answer to a frightened seller turns into a prediction. And we already decided, on purpose, that the product has no open chat. Skip, no trigger.

Do now: thirteen free things

Each one is a page, a setting, or a habit. None is a new feature. Name the IDs you want.

  • X-01Answer threads in the existing groups, weekly3 to 5 suspension threads a week with a plain reading of the poster's notice: what Amazon asks for, the deadline, the records, the scam signals. No link and no product name on Amazon's own forum (it suspends for that). 60 to 90 minutes a week. This month.
  • X-02Make the three email addresses realsupport@, billing@ and privacy@ are on the live site and do not exist. Cloudflare forwards them to one Gmail for free. Needs the real domain first.
  • X-03A private "is the site up?" monitorFree, 15 minutes. Alerts only you. Not the public status page, which stays parked.
  • X-04Ten ready answers and an automatic "we got your message" replyThe ten questions sellers ask, with honest answers, on the support page and saved in Gmail. The exact words for "will I win?" are in the Support part below.
  • X-05A log of every support messageDate, type, minutes. In 60 days it replaces our guess about support time with a real number.
  • X-06A help page with a search boxEvery FAQ question, the five guides and the support limits in one place, searchable, inside our own site. Free, one session.
  • X-07A "How we test" page with our test notices sharedOur results with dates, including our misses. The made-up test notices published for anyone to try. A place for writers and developers to report a miss. Never the engine itself. Read the note about the public code first
  • X-08A short "how your data is protected" sectionOnly what is true today. Plus the standard file that tells security researchers where to write.
  • X-09Tidier emailsRebuild the purchase and reminder emails on a free template kit, and add the missing line "one case, every round" to the purchase email.
  • X-10A page listing the free toolsThe way the big tools do it. After the free doors from the growth plan exist.
  • X-11A booking link for talking to writers and VAsFree. Used only in your outreach messages, "20 minutes about the tool". Never on the support or pricing page, where it would look like talking to a person about a case.
  • X-12Switch the visitor counter onOne setting on Vercel. Until then no channel can be measured.
  • X-13Your own page, in your own wordsWho builds this and why. No AI writes it. The one-line sentence for the home page is part of this.

Skip, with the reason

Written down so nobody researches them again.

Community shapes (5)
  • A forum on our own siteCold start against groups of 52,000 to 1.2 million; legal duties for hosted posts; a new public surface before 50 cases.
  • Our own subreddit or Facebook groupEmpty next to the ones that exist. Use those.
  • A paid community or membership$89 a month with no free plan, and it is exactly what Paddle bans.
  • "Experts answer" anywhere; an AI bot answeringSee the Experts part above.
  • Letting a community share invoice "sources" or insider-contact tipsOur rules route those cases to professional help. One warning line is the limit.
Support shapes (6)
  • A live-chat bubbleIt promises an instant reply one person cannot give. The free chat tools either process in the US or lack an email channel, and one sells human agents by the hour.
  • A public "ask AppealDeck" AI boxIt would put a wrong promise in front of a frightened seller at scale.
  • Building our own email intake in codeTwo days of work for what a Gmail filter does.
  • Promising 24/7, same day, or "within hours"; moving support to WhatsAppOne person answers. A missed promise is the one-star pattern in this market. And our own scam check flags a move to WhatsApp as a warning sign.
  • Giving guidance on arbitration or court stepsThe Terms say where we stop. One line pointing to a lawyer.
  • Drafting replies with the free AI keyGoogle's free terms allow training and human review of the text.
Reach shapes (8)
  • Links or our name on Amazon's forum; promotion on Reddit outside the one allowed postSuspensions of 1 to 30 days, then permanent. "Immediate ban, no exceptions."
  • Product Hunt or Hacker NewsAmazon-seller tools get 2 to 127 upvotes there; four posts ever on Hacker News, 1 to 10 points. Wrong room.
  • YouTube Shorts as a channelThe one agency that grew on YouTube made about 2,400 videos over years.
  • A newsletter nowNo audience yet; a weekly promise by one person. Waits as X-19.
  • One SEO page per notice type from a templateGoogle's rules name this as spam. A few thin pages drag the whole small site down.
  • Open-sourcing the engine; an AI "skill" that writes the appealA copycat could ship our decoder under another name. A drafted appeal outside our fact check is the "copy and paste job" sellers hate.
  • Paid ads in months 1 to 3The maths was already shown closed on 21 September.
  • Co-branding with Extreme Commerce; saying Amazon's own assistant "does nothing" for suspended sellers; asking for reviews before the profiles are claimedReputation risk; an unproven claim about Amazon; and a review profile that cannot be answered.
Tool shapes (5)
  • A public roadmap or feedback boardYour parked-items file is the roadmap. A public board collects requests you have decided not to do yet.
  • A separate help-center hostSplits the site and skips the copy checks that keep "guarantee" out.
  • Paid search services; two tour libraries that need a paid licenceFree alternatives do the job.
  • Legal-page generatorsThey would replace the hand-written sentences our tests pin and the regulators require.
  • Self-hosting support or newsletter servers; paying for a design kitServers to patch for a one-person team; our own design system already exists.

Where the research disagrees with your decisions of this morning

Your decision stands. This is written down so it is not argued again by accident.

  • The scam check on its own pageYou said skip. Two agents ranked it the second-best reason for any seller to visit once, because fake "Amazon" emails reach healthy sellers too, and a plain page name can be typed into forums that ban links. If you ever reopen one of the three, this is the strongest case.
  • The "What we will not do" pageYou said skip. The agents called it the one trust page none of the eight competitors has. Under your "lead with strengths" idea, those lines live in the Terms and on the support page instead, so nothing is hidden.
  • The free supplier-letter pageYou said skip. The agents ranked it sixth: the most-forwarded document in the whole process. It stays inside the case for now.

Support in months 1 to 3

Email only. You answer, inside the two business days the site already promises.

  1. Tools: the three addresses forwarded to one Gmail; labels; the ten ready answers saved as templates; one automatic acknowledgement per thread.
  2. Hours: one fixed 45 to 60 minute block every working day, Pakistan time. That holds up to about 50 messages a month.
  3. Every reply ends the same way: "Amazon decides the outcome and the review time." and "This is software support, not legal advice, and nothing you send here is privileged."
  4. Refund: same day, the 7-day policy. Fraud or forged-document cases: the professional-help line only. Arbitration or court: one line, a lawyer.
  5. Away for more than two business days? Change the support page and the banner before you go. Both must stay true.
  6. At day 60: the log decides whether two business days stays or becomes five.

What you say when asked "will I win?"

I can't tell you that, and I won't estimate it. Nobody outside Amazon can; Amazon decides the outcome and the review time. What I can do is check that your response answers what the notice asked for: the records Amazon named, what went wrong, what you changed, and the dates. Paste the notice's request list and tell me which records you have, and I'll tell you what is still missing. Work to the date on your notice; a reply here cannot extend it, so do not wait on me if it is close. This is software support, not legal advice, and nothing you send here is privileged.

Never add a timeline, a percentage, "most sellers", "good chance" or "should be fine". In this market the review score follows whether a named person replies, not whether the appeal won: the one-star reviews of the big firms are about silence; the five-star ones praise fast replies from a named person.

The honest numbers

Months 1 to 3, no domain, no spend, 2 to 3 hours a week: tens to low hundreds of visits a month, almost all from threads and conversations you started. Decodes in the tens. Cases in single digits.

The ceiling set by others after years: Riverbend about 5,600 visits a month. The funded AI rival AppealsPro is too small for the traffic tools to show. The first paid Pass will not come from Google in this window; it comes from writers running live cases with you.

Your 2 to 3 hours a week: 60 to 90 minutes answering threads (X-01); 60 minutes with writers and VAs in Lahore and Karachi; 15 minutes on the one allowed Reddit post, then only helpful answers there; monthly, the fresh-wording check with the miss count published.

Two findings to act on 1. Our GitHub repository is public. Checked today from this machine: anyone can read the engine code and every commit message, even though the licence says all rights reserved. Advice: make it private. (Free private repositories get 2,000 minutes of automated checks a month; check our usage first.)
2. The visitor counter (Plausible) is wired but off until one setting is filled in on Vercel. Nothing in this plan can be measured before that.

Only you can decide these

  1. The public code repository. Keep it public on purpose, or make it private (advice: private).
  2. Which of X-01 to X-13 to do. Name them, or say "do X-01 to X-13". A general "go ahead" starts nothing.
  3. The three items you struck this morning. They stay struck unless you reopen one by name.
  4. The support window. Keep two business days only if you will read the mailbox every working day; otherwise change the page to five before launch.
  5. Urdu. Only if you will answer in Urdu.
  6. The product name in the "copy for the forum" text. Advice: leave it out on Amazon's forum.
  7. A human review, ever. Only after 50 cases, and only after asking Paddle in writing.
