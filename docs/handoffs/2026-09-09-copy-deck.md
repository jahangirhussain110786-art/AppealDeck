## 0. What stays fixed (the lines we sell inside)

- **No "guarantee", no win or success rates, no percentages, no "N hours".** These are D6 and the `lint-copy` gate; they are also what separates us from the agencies that over-promise.
- **No fake urgency, no fake social proof.** No countdowns, no "12 sellers bought today", no testimonials until they are real and opted in.
- **The expectations card stays at the point of purchase and inside the app** (D6). It moves off the home page. Its wording is softened (§4.8) but it still says who decides.
- **The independence line stays in the footer, Terms and FAQ** (AM-19, trademark law). It comes off the hero and the link-preview image.
- **Legal pages, the ratified FAQ answer "Is AppealDeck part of Amazon?", `SAMPLE_POA` and the sample notice are not touched by this deck.**
- Every number we use has a source in the product or the policy: $199, 7-day refund (D8), 90-day default appeal window and the ~60-day funds-appeal point (already in the FAQ and the deadlines model), 5 devices, 10 MB per file.

## 1. Positioning

**One line:** AppealDeck turns an Amazon notice into a clear, evidence-backed Plan of Action — in your browser, on your terms.

**Category we claim:** appeal-preparation software. Not an agency, not a "reinstatement service", not a chat bot. This is the honest position *and* the strong one: the agencies sell outcomes they do not control; we sell the thing the seller does control — the quality of the appeal.

**The reader:** a seller who has just lost their income, is angry and frightened, and has three questions in this order: *What does this notice mean? How long do I have? What do I write?* Every surface answers those three, in that order.

**The promise we can keep:** clarity, structure, control, speed, a fair deal. The word "reinstatement" appears only in the FAQ answer about who decides.

## 3. Voice rules

1. **Lead with the outcome the seller controls.** "Draft a Plan of Action Amazon can act on" — not "we can't promise anything".
2. **State boundaries as control, not incapacity.** "You submit it yourself in Seller Central" (control). Never "we are unable to submit".
3. **One truth, once, in the right place.** Who decides appeals is said in the FAQ and on the expectations card at purchase — not in the hero, the footer, the meta description or the link image.
4. **Specific beats superlative.** Name the three POA sections, the evidence checklist, the critic. Never "powerful", "seamless", "effortless", "instantly", "AI-powered", "peace of mind", "hassle-free", "secure" (all lint-banned or soft-banned).
5. **Verbs on buttons.** "Decode my notice — free", "Get the Appeal Pass", "Continue to purchase".
6. **Calm punctuation.** No exclamation marks. Sentences under twenty words on marketing surfaces.
7. **Honest urgency only.** The deadline in the seller's own notice is real; we point at it. We never manufacture one.

## 6. Acquisition levers that fit inside D6 and the feature freeze

- **The free decoder is the funnel** (D10). Every marketing surface points at it first; the Pass is offered after the seller has seen a real result about their own case.
- **The deadline is the urgency.** "See how long you have" is a stronger and truer hook than any timer.
- **Mechanism as proof.** The hero shows a real decode; the badges are checkable; the policy-checked stamp shows currency. This is what replaces testimonials until real ones exist.
- **Price framing: one payment, one case, refund.** The 2026 tool wave is all-subscription (2 Sep research); "once" is our differentiator and it is true.
- **FAQ as the long-tail SEO surface** (spec 06 §7.1). The new questions ("How long does it take?", "What if Amazon replies?") are the searches a suspended seller actually types.
- Parked until after the freeze (not this pass): per-notice-type landing pages, a shareable decode summary link, an opt-in outcomes page.
