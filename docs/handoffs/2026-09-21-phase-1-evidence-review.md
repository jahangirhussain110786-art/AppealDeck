# Phase 1: facts that should shape AppealDeck

Checked: 21 September 2026. Repository: `52cf0cf`. Status: Phase 1 complete within its agreed scope; unresolved claims remain explicitly unresolved. Research and source inspection only. No app changes or runtime validation.

## Plain-language result

The earlier research supports a useful direction: help the seller understand the actual request, collect the right evidence, prepare an appropriate response, and manage the next reply. It does not prove that this will be a winning paid product. That is the question for Phase 2.

Three corrections matter most:

1. **Build on what exists.** AppealDeck already has workspace routing, manual evidence review, document-version checks, submission history, and a detailed text export. The missing pieces are narrower than a complete rebuild.
2. **Amazon is already helping with appeals.** Its own staff confirms step-by-step Seller Assistant guidance and possible task assistance. We must test what extra value sellers need; neither “Amazon cannot help” nor “Amazon has solved the whole problem” is established. [Amazon's account-health Q&A](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/21ef8989-82f3-4c09-a548-a6f1d3ed2cad)
3. **Do not turn AI speculation into product rules.** The selected sources do not substantiate the transcript's rejection percentages, approval odds, universal word counts, or bot-scoring claims. Several real requirements have narrower scope than earlier summaries implied.

My recommendation from this phase is to retain the evidence-led case-workflow hypothesis, correct the research baseline, and test demand before approving the proposed feature expansion.

## Method and limits

Reused the founder prompt, v2 planning documents, paused-research handoff, and relevant saved Amazon-mechanics excerpts. Ran six search queries, made 12 distinct original-page retrieval attempts, and used targeted lookups within those pages. Ten pages were readable; the full BSA required sign-in and the Agent Policy retrieval failed. No subagents, paid product API calls, interviews, builds, or tests.

All verdicts below were checked on the date above. Official announcements establish what Amazon announced; staff forum guidance has contextual limits; seller posts establish individual reports, not general rules. Several pages are older guidance retrieved today, not new September 2026 announcements. No live Seller Central account was tested. “Unsupported” means this bounded review did not establish a claim, not that the opposite has been proven.

## Decisions this evidence supports

These are recommendations, not implementation approvals:

1. **Keep the actual notice and response form as the basis for guidance.** Show what was extracted, what the seller confirmed, and what remains uncertain. A useful first screen can propose a route without claiming certainty before the needed information exists.
2. **Prioritize observable help over prediction.** Finding a missing page or unresolved request can be demonstrated. Predicting approval or impersonating Amazon's internal reviewer cannot be justified by this evidence.
3. **Extend existing case infrastructure.** Evaluate document-reading assistance, reminders, reply comparison, and evidence packaging as additions to working structures. Do not cost or plan them as if none of the underlying case work exists.
4. **Treat integrations and additional case types as optional expansions pending evidence.** Verify real demand and access before adding a large dependency or changing specialist boundaries.
5. **Test differentiation instead of declaring it.** Independent case records and continuity are plausible value propositions. This phase does not establish that competitors lack them or that sellers will pay for them.

## What remains open

- Current Seller Assistant behavior for the exact case types and account states AppealDeck would serve.
- Precise current INFORM certification triggers and deadlines; use actual notices until verified.
- Full current BSA/Agent Policy interpretation for the proposed access and data uses. Provider retention/privacy terms, professional-service boundaries, and cross-jurisdiction legal guidance were not audited here.
- General causal evidence about duplicate appeals, model-written text, rejection rates, or successful outcomes. No numerical success claims are approved by this research.
- Routing/extraction accuracy on a representative, permissioned notice corpus. No corpus or benchmark was built in this phase.

## Five questions for Phase 2

1. Which narrowly defined seller situation has the greatest unmet need and is realistically within AppealDeck's supported scope?
2. What specific job remains difficult after using Seller Assistant, Seller Central, and available alternatives?
3. Do sellers most value evidence preparation, understanding the request, managing revisions, or access to professional review—and what evidence supports their willingness to pay?
4. What response times, case coverage, privacy assurances, and support expectations would make the purchase trustworthy and sustainable?
5. What observable experience would cause a seller to recommend AppealDeck even if Amazon does not reinstate the account?

Phase 2 is not started. The next pass should use the saved seller-voice and agency research selectively, compare a small number of relevant alternatives, and distinguish reported pain from validated demand.
