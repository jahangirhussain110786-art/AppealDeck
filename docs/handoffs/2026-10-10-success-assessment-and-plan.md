# AppealDeck: honest success assessment and the plan from A to Z

**Date:** 10 October 2026
**Who this is for:** the founder (Jhangir Hussain), solo, non-developer, Pakistan
**What it is:** four research angles (market, product, competition and distribution, operations and money) plus a red-team pass that tried to break every claim. Nothing here is a decision. Decisions are listed in section 8 for you.

**How to read the numbers.** Every number carries a tag:

- **V** = verified today or earlier this month against a page or the code, source given.
- **E** = estimated, with the basis given. Treat an E as a planning number, not a fact.
- **U** = unverified: a claim someone made that nobody could check.

**Two rules kept throughout (D6):** no win rate is claimed anywhere, and the word that promises an outcome is not used.

## 1. The short answer

The product is real, unusually complete for a solo build, and the people it serves are in real pain. That part is good and it is not small.

But whether it ever pays you is decided by three things, and none of them is code:

1. **A card must be chargeable.** Paddle is your only payment rail. The red team checked the fallbacks today: Dodo Payments excludes Pakistan, Lemon Squeezy pays "rest of world" by PayPal only (not available in Pakistan), Polar uses Stripe Connect (not in Pakistan). If Paddle says no, there is no plan C right now.
2. **Someone must be sent to the site.** Search takes 6 to 12 months on a domain that is not registered yet, and most new pages never reach page one. The only fast door is people you reach yourself: appeal writers and VAs in Lahore and Karachi.
3. **A stranger must pay $249 once for software.** Nobody in this market has ever been seen to do that. The slot is empty. It may be empty because it is open, or because it does not work. Only sales will tell.

**Honest odds (E, judgment from the funnel, the base rates and the risks above):**

| Milestone                                       | Odds                                                                                                                  |
| ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| First paid Pass within 60 days of a real launch | 25 to 40% (40 to 55% if you personally bring 10 to 20 writers or VAs to live cases; 10 to 20% if you wait for Google) |
| 10 paid Passes in one month, within 6 months    | 10 to 20%                                                                                                             |
| About $10k gross a month by month 12 to 18      | 3 to 8%                                                                                                               |
| Still online and still correct after 2 years    | 25 to 40%                                                                                                             |
| Zero sales in the first 6 months                | about 40 to 50%                                                                                                       |

**The one thing that matters most:** get a human being to the site in the first 60 days instead of waiting for search. Everything else in this plan is arranged around that.

Expect zero income for several months. Keep your other income.

## 3. The hard parts, ranked

Ranked by how much each one can stop the money, not by how hard it is to fix.

### 1. Paddle must approve the site, and it is the only rail (fatal if it fails)

- **Why hard:** Paddle lists "legal advice" under prohibited human services and "reserves the right to exercise judgment" (**V**, Paddle AUP). A competitor's article names "no processing history," "incomplete-looking site," "identity mismatch" and "tightening around some generative-AI products" as common refusal reasons (**U**, written by a Paddle competitor). Your pricing page describes AI drafting and AI document reading. Pakistan is not on Paddle's unsupported list (**V**).
- **No plan C today (V):** Dodo's own accepted-countries page excludes Pakistan. Lemon Squeezy pays rest-of-world by PayPal only; PayPal is not in Pakistan. Polar pays through Stripe Connect; Stripe does not serve Pakistan.
- **Cost:** about $10 for the domain (**V**, Cloudflare $10.46/year), one day of copy work, 2 to 10 business days of review.
- **The way through:** buy appealdeck.com, deploy there, put Terms, Refund and Privacy links in the navigation, name "Jhangir Hussain" in the Terms (already there), change the one phrase "an independent service" on /pricing to "software" (**V**, seen on the live page), and make every public page read as software the seller operates. Then submit. If refused, ask for the written reason, fix only that, re-apply once. In parallel, ask Payoneer whether its request-a-payment billing can take USD card payments from partner or agency clients (**U**).
- **Odds of first-try approval (E):** a coin flip. Nobody publishes an approval rate.

### 2. Nobody has ever paid $249 once for self-serve appeal software (hard)

- **Why hard:** the only one-time tool ever found ($11, AppealPath) is dead (**V**). Buyers are trained on free letters (AppealsPro), $50 to $70 Fiverr humans, and $950+ firms with free re-appeals (**V**, Thompson & Holt £700 includes further appeals). A seller in panic may prefer a person.
- **Cost:** $0 in money; 3 to 6 months of traffic, or weeks if you bring writers to live cases.
- **The way through:** reach 50 cases started, count Passes. Under 1 in 50, do not build features; test a documents-only lower price, a human-review add-on with a vetted writer, or a per-seat writer licence. Decide at 100 cases, not before.

### 3. No seller access, and the writer channel needs a price that does not exist (hard)

- **Why hard:** you have no direct access to sellers or appeal writers (recorded 22 Sep). The research found your real asset: Pakistan hosts the largest Amazon-seller training communities (Extreme Commerce, Enablers, **V**) and many Fiverr/Upwork reinstatement writers (**V**, listings at $15 to $70 an hour). But those writers sell a POA for about $50 to $70 (**V**). A writer cannot pay $249 per case out of a $50 gig. The channel is real only if the writer's client pays, or you price a partner licence. The 21 Sep review deferred that to P2.
- **Cost:** 2 to 3 hours a week, in person or in Urdu. No money.
- **The way through:** ten conversations in 30 days with writers and VA agencies in Lahore and Karachi. Ask what they use today (Word, WhatsApp, a sheet), whether they would run one live case through the tool this month, and what they would pay per month. Count a buyer only when a Pass is paid.

### 4. The product-safety guidance now sends some sellers the wrong way (hard, and the one case of harmful advice found)

- **Why hard:** `src/core/evidenceModel.ts` tells a product-safety seller to produce "a test report or compliance certificate." Four approved labs (Intertek, SGS, Eurofins, STC/HQTS) describe Amazon's Direct Validation program: for toys (US/CA/EU), supplements, batteries and others, the lab submits results directly through Account Health > Policy Compliance > Verify Your Products, and self-uploaded reports are "no longer accepted" (**V**, the labs' own pages; Amazon's help text still not found). A grep of `src/` for "TIC," "Verify Your Product" or "direct validation" finds nothing (**V**).
- **Cost:** half a day.
- **The way through:** change the wording to "Amazon may require testing through one of its approved labs, which submits the result directly; look for a Verify Your Products button under Policy Compliance," with a dated source. Pin it with a test.

### 5. Trust: unknown solo founder from Pakistan in a market with a bribery and scam history (hard)

- **Why hard:** Bloomberg (24 Jun 2026) describes an active bribery market preying on suspended sellers (**V**). DOJ cases 2020 to 2023 (**V**). Human firms have 280 to 1,303 dated reviews (**V**, Trustpilot, Feefo); you have zero. The live site is on a free vercel.app subdomain with no founder page and no walkthrough.
- **Cost:** your time. A Trustpilot business profile is free.
- **The way through:** show mechanism instead of social proof. A founder page in your own words (F-10), a 3-minute walkthrough (F-11), mailboxes that exist (F-05), the two-business-day window actually met, the researched-notices test report published as a page, claimed Trustpilot and Google profiles showing zero honestly, a public opt-in outcome ledger that starts at "0 of 0 reported" and always shows the denominator.

### 6. Google may not bill a Pakistani card for Gemini (hard)

- **Why hard:** Pakistan is absent from Google Cloud's supported-currency table (**V**). Google rejects prepaid cards, virtual cards and debit cards that need two-factor authentication (**V**), which covers many Pakistani cards. The key is currently on the free tier: 20 requests a day per model (**V**, the 429 error text). The privacy policy promises the paid tier (D9).
- **Cost:** $20 to $50 prepaid credit, one hour.
- **The way through:** try a non-prepaid, non-virtual Visa or Mastercard with international payments on. If declined: Google support, then a reseller. If still blocked: remove AI drafting and AI document reading from the pricing page and sell the device reader plus the seller's own fact-locked wording; re-pin the legal disclosures test to the new truth. The product still works without AI.

### 7. Free substitutes and Amazon's own assistant (hard, slow-moving)

- **Why hard:** AppealsPro's free tier (10 notice analyses a day plus letter generation, **V**), SellerForge's free POA generator (**V**), and a free MIT-licensed "amazon-suspension-appeal" skill for Claude Code and Cursor (744 stars, **V**). Mr Jeff's free diagnostic is a gated teaser, not a full equivalent (**V**, red team). Amazon's Seller Assistant monitors account health, reads documents, runs workflows that "draft a response plan," and staff say it "may" help with appeal tasks (**V**). Nothing Amazon announced covers deactivated accounts, Plans of Action or reinstatement (**V**, verified absence).
- **The way through:** position on what Amazon will not build for a seller it just removed: the case record across rounds, the invoice check, the deadline clock, the reply reader, help after deactivation. Pre-write the one-page "what we sell the day Amazon drafts POAs" and keep it in the repo. Fifteen minutes a month watching Seller Assistant release notes.

### 9. Non-US stores (moderate; the "dead end" is already fixed)

- **Correction (V):** the red team found the code no longer dead-ends a non-US store. `routeWorkspace()` sends it down the same route and adds a notice that the guidance is written for Amazon US (`src/core/workspace.ts` lines 978 to 981, `src/content/stores.ts`). The 29 Sep report describes the old behaviour.
- **What is still true:** guidance, deadlines and addresses are US-written. EU/EEA/UK purchases are blocked by default (F-08). A quarter of one competitor's visitors are in the UK; India, Turkey and Pakistan together are another 30 to 45% (**V**, Similarweb). Many of those sell on Amazon.com, which fits.
- **The way through:** say "written for Amazon US" before the paste box, not only on /pricing. Later, a sourced per-store table for the English stores (CA, AU, IN, SA, AE). Keep EU/UK blocked until F-08 is decided.

### 10. Support load (moderate)

- **Why hard:** every buyer is in crisis and will write. /support promises two business days (**V**). No public benchmark for support time per case; 30 to 90 minutes per paid case is the planning figure (**E**). Three mailboxes named on live pages do not exist yet (**V**, F-05).
- **The way through:** create the mailboxes (free forwarding is enough). Ten canned answers. One fixed hour a day for the ops digest and support. If two business days cannot be held, change the page to five.

### 11. Refunds and chargebacks at tiny volume (moderate)

- **Why hard:** most first appeals fail industry-wide; a $249 buyer refused by Amazon inside the 7-day window will ask for the money back. Planning range 10 to 25% refunds (**E**, no data). One chargeback in the first 15 sales is 6.6%, ten times Paddle's 0.65% threshold (**V**). A chargeback costs $20 plus the sale, fee kept even on a win (**V**).
- **The way through:** refund within hours, never argue. Log every reason. Keep `RESEND_API_KEY` live so the D8 confirmation email exists as evidence. Keep fraud and forged-document cases routed away (D6); those buyers dispute most.

### 12. Key-person risk (hard, slow)

- **Why hard:** one non-developer founder building through AI sessions is the only operator and holds every credential. Frameworks need a major upgrade about yearly (Next 14 to 16 already happened). Solo founders with no revenue by month 12 usually stop. CI has gone red unnoticed twice in this repo's history (**V**).
- **The way through:** a sealed continuity note this month (where every secret lives, the backup passphrase, how to refund all open Passes, how to switch the site to a read-only decoder). A password manager with emergency access for one trusted person. Calendar the monthly routine; two missed months in a row is the early signal.

### 13. Amazon's review silence (minor, copy only)

Amazon says 3 to 7 business days, not promised; sellers report 10 days to 2+ months (**V**, forums). A seller will judge the tool by Amazon's silence. State the range on the dashboard and give them something to do while waiting (the product partly does).

## 8. Decisions for the founder

Each one lists the options and a recommendation. Nothing here is decided until you say so.

1. **If Paddle refuses twice.** Options: (a) keep the site as a free decoder and build reputation until a rail opens; (b) ask Payoneer about request-a-payment billing for partner and agency clients only (**U**); (c) find a trusted person in a Paddle-supported country to be the merchant (needs a written agreement and changes who the legal seller is). **Recommendation:** try (a) plus (b) together; do not do (c) without a lawyer.

2. **A partner or writer licence.** The writer channel is your only fast door and writers cannot pay $249 per case out of a $50 gig. Options: (a) client pays $249, writer gets a partner payout (say 20 to 30%, in line with seller-tool affiliate norms, **V**); (b) a per-seat monthly writer licence (SellerForge prices agency tooling at $4,990 a year, **V**; something like $49 to $99 a month per writer is the band); (c) both. **Recommendation:** start with (a) because it needs only a code at checkout and a monthly Payoneer payout; move to (b) after Phase 1 shows 3 or more writers running cases. The 21 Sep review put the agency tier at P2; this research moves it up.

3. **If Google will not bill the card.** Options: (a) Google support, then a reseller; (b) another AI provider, which rewrites the privacy and Terms sentences about Google and re-pins the disclosures test; (c) sell without AI: device reader plus the seller's own fact-locked wording, and remove AI drafting from the pricing page. **Recommendation:** (a) for two weeks, then (c), because (b) is a week of legal-copy work for an untested provider.

4. **The product-safety wording change.** Approve changing `evidenceModel.ts` PRODUCT_SAFETY to the Direct Validation wording with a dated source. **Recommendation:** approve this week; it is the one verified case of advice that could cost a seller the case.

5. **Non-US scope.** Options: (a) "written for Amazon US" above the paste box and nothing more; (b) a sourced per-store table for CA, AU, IN, SA, AE; (c) open UK/EU (needs F-08 first). **Recommendation:** (a) now, (b) in Phase 3, (c) only after F-08.

6. **EU/UK (F-08): comply or decline.** GDPR applies with no size threshold once EU sellers are served; the free decoder is already reachable from the EU. **Recommendation:** decline for now (keep purchases blocked), revisit when there are 20 paying cases and a lawyer read.

7. **Support window.** Two business days (as written) or five. **Recommendation:** keep two only if you will read the mailbox every working day; otherwise change the page to five before launch. A missed promise is itself a policy breach.

8. **Sign out scope (F-12).** End only this device's session (`scope: "local"`, the usual default) or every device (today). **Recommendation:** local; it matches what sellers expect and removes the shared-account test flake.

9. **SP-API (R-9).** Read-only early warning plus verified outcomes, or stay a no-access tool. It reopens D7, changes the "no access" line, registers you as a Solution Provider and makes the product an Agent under the policy. **Recommendation:** not before 20 paying cases; the "no access" line is a sales asset today.

10. **The micro ad test.** Only after the Keyword Planner hour. **Recommendation:** run it once at a $100 to $200 cap only if long-tail CPC is under about $4; if $8 or more, close ads for good and stop debating it.

11. **Price-shape tests at the 50-case gate.** Options in order: documents-only lower price; human-review add-on with a vetted writer (needs D5's collaborator agreement first); writer licence. **Recommendation:** pre-agree the order now so the gate does not become a month of discussion.

12. **The AI second reading on the anonymous /decode page and the reply reader** (D9 spend on visitors with no Pass, open since 9 Oct). **Recommendation:** keep the 3-a-day cap until billing is on and the R-2 counters show it is kept more than changed; then raise it.

13. **Incorporation trigger.** D4 says about 100 paying users or PKR 5 to 10M retained profit. **Recommendation:** no change.

14. **Lawyer read budget** ($300 to $1,500, **E**). **Recommendation:** after Paddle approval and before any marketing push, not before.

15. **Three questions for a Pakistani tax adviser before the first payout:** do Paddle-via-Payoneer receipts count as IT export proceeds under section 154A; what the PSEB fee and the 80% remittance condition are today; whether filing as an individual with the Hawlton trading name is enough until D4's threshold. **Recommendation:** ask before the first payout, not after.

16. **The monthly routine and emergency access.** Who holds the password-manager emergency access, and whether the monthly routine is a calendar event you will keep. **Recommendation:** name one person this month and put the routine on the calendar for 24 months.

17. **What you will not build until 50 cases have started.** **Recommendation:** nothing. Every number in this document says the problem is sales, not features.
