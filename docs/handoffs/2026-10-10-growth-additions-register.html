0The short answer

What a first-time visitor gets today (V, product walk). A calm, serious page with the paste box above the fold on a phone. One tap after pasting, the answer: _"Amazon wants a Plan of Action."_, the problem, the deadline (or "No date stated"), a scam check, the notice marked sentence by sentence, the records to gather, and do-now / do-not cards. Under a second on the server. No account. No score, no fake reviews, no ticker. That first minute is already better than AppealsPro, Mr Jeff or SellerForge (section 1, "competitors").

Then it fades. The result is thirteen phone screens long. The next button says "Open case workspace". Two questions a frightened seller has are left open: _how long do I have when no date is written_ and _what happens to my money_. A VA with twelve clients cannot name a case. A writer finds the exports on the last tab. And a paying buyer copies "## Root Cause" into Amazon's box.

The three or four changes that would move the experience most (E, ranked by the red team's chain from first visit to felt value to word of mouth):

1

Answer the two first questions on the first screen

A "Your money" line when the notice mentions held funds (the parser already flags it and nothing reads it, V), and a truthful deadline line instead of "ambiguous" (W-01, A-23).

2

One tap to a full sample result

, so an evaluator or a VA judges in ten seconds without pasting (B-08).

3

Open two more free doors nobody else has:

paste Amazon's _reply_ and see refused / documents asked / reinstated (A-03); and the invoice check showing its arithmetic, days and units, with the working (A-18).

4

Sell the record, not the draft

Hero sub-line and the free-plan list: what Amazon asked, what you sent, what came back, every round, and "never send the same appeal twice" (A-37, A-56).

What we can give free that nobody gives free (V, competitor pages fetched 10 Oct): reading Amazon's reply; invoice arithmetic against 365 days and units sold; a scam check that names its signals; the held-funds clock with its policy date; a supplier letter; video-verification preparation; and a published record of our own misses.

What we must not do: show a score or a rate, promise an outcome, touch the seller's Amazon account, or fix a first-screen defect with a sentence that is not true (D6, AM-27).
