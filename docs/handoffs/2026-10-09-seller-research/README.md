## 0. The brief, as corrected

The founder's wording asked for "the tools sellers mostly use". Taken literally that lists Helium 10, Jungle Scout and Keepa, which sell market data we do not have and should not chase. The useful question is narrower:

> Which task in a seller's or VA's day has no good free home, can be done entirely from what they paste, upload or type, costs us nothing per use, and makes a professional think "this is serious" at first glance?

Everything below answers that question under three hard limits: no Seller Central reading or automation (AM-27, Amazon's Agent Policy binds the seller); zero marginal cost (rules, the seller's own data, or a free public source; no paid API); and honest framing (D6: nothing predicts an outcome, nothing is called "compliant").

## 1. The plain-words answer

**A suspension is an episode. A seller's day is a routine. We cannot be the routine, but we can be the first thing opened when the routine finds a problem, and for a VA that is every day.**

What the research settled:

1. **The day starts with Account Health, then Performance Notifications, then suppressed and stranded listings, then cases.** This order is stated by sellers (Amazon's own compilation of 500+ seller replies, updated ≈May 2026: "The first thing I do every morning is check my account health"), by an agency describing its routine for every client account, and by two VA agencies' SOPs. A real seller's July 2026 post lists the ten tabs kept open all day. (File A, §1.)
2. **We cannot be that morning page, and neither can any paid tool now.** Amazon's own Seller Assistant monitors account health free (17 Sep 2025) and since 23 Sep 2026 runs "account health" workflows while the seller is logged out. Seller Labs' CEO: "It took the easy part of what we sold." Monitoring is Amazon's now, and reading the account is forbidden to us anyway. (File B, §4.)
3. **What Amazon still does not do, verified across every 2025–2026 Amazon source:** decode a notice into what kind of response it needs and by when; judge whether an invoice will pass; read the seller's own text or documents _before_ submission; or help anyone already deactivated. Its protections (Account Health Assurance, Seller Challenge) start at AHR 250 and skip everyone below. That is our ground and nobody is standing on it. (File B §4, File C §9.)
4. **The people who touch notifications daily are VAs and agency account managers, not most sellers.** One agency manager runs 10–15 brand accounts; five 2024–2026 VA job posts ($600–$1,200 a month) all pair "monitor account health daily" with "documentation requests", "suppressed ASINs", "appeals/POAs", and one asks the VA to "centralize and maintain an up-to-date library of all regulatory, technical, and compliance-related documents". Agencies keep a **"suppression log with follow-up dates"** in Airtable or Google Sheets. (File A, §2.) That log is what we should be.
5. **The paid services sellers resent most are, underneath, computation on things the seller already has.** Riverbend Guardian reads notifications daily "from $17/day" (≈$510 a month). Invoice review is folded into $950–$2,300 appeal fees, or sold at $75 an invoice. Listing restricted-word checks cost $59–$199 a month (Perci) or $99 per ASIN (keywords.am). Reimbursement finders take 20–25% of money Amazon now often pays automatically. No free tool computes a seller's own deadlines from their own dates. (File B §1.5, §1.10, §2; File C ranked table.)
6. **The "gem at first glance" is not more features.** It is that every answer shows its arithmetic and its source with a date ("this window was 60 days until 16 Feb 2026, now 30"), needs no sign-up, and answers in one screen. The SAFE-T window was enforced by Amazon's system before Amazon's own help page changed, and an Amazon staffer told a seller the old rule in the forum; a tool that is right about _that_ earns a professional's trust in a way a dashboard never does. (File C, §6.)

**Avoid, with the reason written down so the next session does not pick them up:**

- **Account health monitoring or alerts.** Needs account access (forbidden) and Amazon now does it free.
- **Automated policy-change tracker.** Seller Central news is login-gated; fetching Amazon pages by machine breaches its Conditions of Use; free newsletters cover it. The parked P-01 changelog, human-written and dated, is the honest version.
- **Hazmat classifier.** Chemistry, not rules.
- **SAFE-T / A-to-z templates.** Free commodity on the forums; only the deadline arithmetic (#3) has value.
- **Anything that writes to Amazon** (filing, sending, pricing). D6 and AM-27.

## 4. One bigger bet, for the founder only

**A free reimbursement finder from uploaded reports.** Upload the Inventory Adjustments, Reimbursements, Customer Returns and Refunds reports; get the rows Amazon owes you and the claim window left on each (60 days since Oct 2024). The seller files the claim; we never touch the account. ReimburseOps ($0 / $19 a month, no API) proves it works as an upload-only tool; Getida and Seller Investigators charge 20–25% and the loudest resentment in the research is "Amazon started doing what they are doing automatically — no need to pay 25%" (Trustpilot, Sep 2025).

Why it is here and not in the ranked list: it is a different product (finance, not compliance), it pulls against the "narrow tool that does one job" position that the research says sellers want, and Amazon's proactive reimbursements (Nov 2024) and manufacturing-cost rule (Mar 2025) are shrinking it. Why it is here at all: it is the one candidate that would make a seller open us **every payout** (fortnightly), it runs at zero cost, and it is the clearest "they charge 25%, here it is free" in the market. A founder decision, not a recommendation.

## 5. What "gem at first glance" means in practice

The first-glance reaction is set by the first screen, and the research says what a professional is checking for:

1. **No sign-up to get the answer.** Every free tool that converts (Keepa, the restricted-words lead magnets) answers first. Our paste box already does; the listing check and clock catalogue must too.
2. **Show the working.** "Invoice dated 3 Nov 2025; notice dated 2 Oct 2026; 333 days: inside the window" beats a green tick. The "loop" thread is sellers who could not see the sum.
3. **Date every rule and name its source.** "30 days, from 16 Feb 2026 (was 60)". The person who knows the SAFE-T window moved before the help page did is the person who has been reading the forums, and that is who a VA trusts.
4. **Say what we cannot know.** "Amazon may phone your supplier; we cannot." "A registered mark in your title is worth checking, not a verdict." Seller Assistant misread a T-shirt as a motorcycle helmet (≈Nov 2025); a tool that admits its limits reads as the more serious one.
5. **Never a score, never a prediction.** D6, and also what the forum regulars themselves warn against ("third party services for appeal letters tend to be generic").
6. **Built for several accounts.** The person who opens a tool daily is running 10–15 of them.

## 6. How a seller or VA finds it (distribution, since the founder has no seller access)

Each free tool is also a search landing page. `2026-09-25-keyword-research.md` already lists the phrases; the candidates map onto them: "amazon invoice requirements" (#4), "amazon restricted keywords list" and "antibacterial claim amazon listing removed" (#2), "safe-t claim 30 days", "a-to-z claim appeal deadline", "funds disbursement appeal 60 days" (#3), "amazon performance notification meaning" (#1). The forums and VA job posts are where the users are; a VA who uses the log for one client brings the agency.

Costs that are not zero and must be said: email reminders (Resend free tier is 100 a day, then $20 a month; browser push and the calendar file cost nothing), and the USPTO key's 60-requests-a-minute ceiling (cache results, never mirror the register).
