# $100M Money Models, read in full and applied to AppealDeck (23 Oct 2026)

Source: the founder's copy of Alex Hormozi's *$100M Money Models* (183 pages, all six sections read; the video trainings it links to were not available). Everything below is my own summary and judgement, not quotation. It builds on `2026-10-22-combined-growth-plan.md`, which used the same author's earlier books from public summaries; where this book is more specific, it says so.

## The book in ten lines

1. A money model is an ordered set of offers, built so that **one customer's first-30-days profit pays for getting and serving at least two more**. Cash, not demand, is what limits a small business.
2. Four offer types, in order: **attraction** (strangers to customers), **upsell** (what to offer next), **downsell** (what to offer after a no), **continuity** (what keeps them paying).
3. Build **one stage at a time, in quarters**. A full model installed at once collapses the business. Make each stage reliable before the next.
4. **A downsell is never the same thing for less.** Change how they pay, or change what they get. Never negotiate the price; customers talk.
5. **Upsell at the moment the previous purchase reveals the next problem**, and make it available straight away.
6. **Credit what they already paid toward the next, dearer thing** (the rollover) rather than refund or lose them.
7. **Start prices low enough to get many yeses and feedback, then raise them until sales fall.**
8. **Refund anyone who asks.** Be transparent, obey the law, and do not sell hard; hard selling is for weak products.
9. Payment terms matter as much as price: annual billing loses far fewer customers than monthly.
10. Sell other people's products (affiliate) to fill gaps in the ladder without delivering them yourself.

## What is worth doing, in order

1. **A credited upgrade from One Response to the Appeal Pass** (rollover). At the moment the server refuses round two, offer the Pass at $249 minus the $99 already paid, as a fixed second Paddle price, exactly as the document review does ($49 off, $200). It closes the hole above, treats a frightened buyer fairly, and is the book's clearest fit. Needs a migration and a price; about the size of what was built yesterday. Honest framing: "the $99 you paid comes off". No countdown or pretend scarcity.
2. **An early-customer price, with a real limit.** The book's strongest advice for the first customers: price low enough to get yeses and learn, then raise. The plan fixes $249 from day one and tests a rise after 50 cases, which is the opposite order. A limited Paddle discount (the founding-code mechanism already exists) for the first 25 Appeal Passes, with an honest counter, gets the first testimonials and outcome reports sooner. Founder decision: it overrides the "price stays $249" line in the combined plan.
3. **Reward both sides of a referral.** The book's giveaway chapter turns on the referrer winning too, and the one retention study found earlier says referred customers stay longer. When the partner system (R-17) is built, make the same code available to ordinary buyers: a $25 credit to the friend and to the seller who referred them, issued after the 14-day window. No cash for good reviews (our partner rules already ban it).
4. **Put the next step and its date at the end of every screen.** The book's "book the next meeting from the meeting" rule is a UI rule for us: the case already shows a next-step card and reminders. Check each screen ends in one named action with a date, and that a sent response ends with "Amazon usually answers in about X; we will remind you on D".
5. **After a case closes, ask for honest feedback, not praise.** The goodwill chapter is just a plain ask. We have the opt-in outcome share and the $25 rebate; add a permission line ("may we quote your words, with your name or without?"). Needed because we have no testimonials and may not invent them.
6. **Pro plan: offer 12 months paid up front** at a discount, and say so next to the monthly price. The book cites a billing-cadence study (monthly billing about 10.7 percent monthly cancellation, annual about 2) and the churn report I read yesterday says cheap monthly plans are worst. The Pro plan is already in the stable price band, so this is the safe way to add continuity.
7. **Fill a gap with someone else's product, with disclosure.** Sellers whose cases we refuse (forged documents, fraud, child safety) are sent to a professional today. A disclosed referral arrangement with a vetted consultant, and an accredited lab for product-safety cases, would add revenue and real help. The risk is a conflict of interest: the advice to seek a professional must never depend on the fee. Do not build until the first writers are verified.

## What not to take from the book, and why

- **Pressure scripts** (provoking "the gasp", assuming the close, getting a "no" to mean yes, asking for the card before explaining terms). They are built for live sales calls to people with money. Our buyers are frightened, our funnel is self-serve, and D6 forbids selling that exploits panic.
- **Giveaways and "free" promotions.** Heavily regulated, and a sweepstakes aimed at suspended sellers would attract the wrong leads.
- **Pay-later with a conditional guarantee, trial with penalty, waived start-up fees, cancellation fees, a fee donated to a cause the customer hates.** Paddle is merchant of record and has no native delayed charge; penalty and cancellation-fee terms are the kind of thing consumer-protection and subscription rules (US, UK) target; and none fits a one-time $99 to $249 purchase.
- **Billing every four weeks instead of monthly** (thirteen charges, not twelve) and **adding a processing fee**. The book is right that they raise revenue; they also look like the tricks sellers' forums warn about, which this brand sells against.
- **Payment plans** at these prices. $249 is small and Paddle has no plan product.
- **Win Your Money Back as written.** It suits products where customers must keep working to reach a result and have a guaranteed outcome to bet on. Ours has no outcome we may promise (D6). The action-based version already exists in small form (the $25 rebate for reporting how a case ended, switched off).

## Two things the book confirms about our own plan

- **Staging.** The book's central warning is not to install a whole model at once. The switched-off Account Pass, packs, Pro plan and rebate were built early; leaving them off until the first stage sells is the right call. Item 1 above is the one exception, because it repairs the step from a product that is about to be on sale.
- **Measuring.** The yardstick is 30-day cash per customer against what it cost to get and serve them, at least twice over. The partner arithmetic in the plan already uses it. Add it to the monthly report (`scripts/pricing-report.mjs`) once there are sales.

## Revisited the same day: the "do not copy" list, on business merit and law

The founder asked for the list to be reconsidered as a business decision, not a moral one: competitors use flattering definitions and every persuasion lever, and a product that does not will lose. Two corrections to my first pass, then the new verdicts. Sources are law-firm advisories and trade press unless noted (labelled as such in the research turn); none replaces counsel.

**Corrections.** (1) I said pressure scripts were forbidden by D6. They are not: D6 names the guarantee ban, the honest-expectations card, severity gating, read-only, local-first and win rates only from opt-in data. Persuasion is allowed; what is not allowed is a claim a buyer cannot check and a regulator can read as false. (2) The decision rule is therefore not "honest or not" but **"can a reasonable buyer be misled, and who can punish it?"** Three parties can: **Paddle** (our only payment rail for a Pakistan-based seller; its terms bar deceptive sales practices, and losing the account ends the business), **the FTC** (the Consumer Review Rule bans fake and sentiment-conditioned reviews with per-violation penalties; Operation AI Comply has run since Sept 2024 against inflated "AI lawyer" and earnings claims, with more than a dozen "AI washing" cases in 2025; ROSCA is the live statute for subscriptions and the 2024 click-to-cancel rule was vacated in July 2025 with a new rulemaking started in 2026), and **sellers' own forums**, which test every claim in this niche. Competitors' 97 percent figures are the exposure we are avoiding, not an edge we are missing.

**Flattering definitions are fine; implied outcomes are not.** Choosing a favourable true metric is ordinary marketing: "cases answered completely: every requested record supplied or declared" is a real, countable thing we can publish once it has a number. It must be labelled as case-file completeness and never read as reinstatement.

**What replaces the success-rate claims** (the levers competitors pull, in forms that survive scrutiny): specific counts of real things (the 13 open test notices, records checked per invoice); process claims (every record Amazon asked for is listed); proof you can download (`/how-we-test`); a named human (the founder story, F-10); risk reversal (the 14-day refund is already stronger than most); a real, counted limit on the early offer; a **measured** speed claim (state the median decode time only after measuring it); and fear of loss told truthfully (the clock above). Social proof once it exists, with disclosure of any free Pass or rebate (the Consumer Review Rule bans rewards conditioned on a positive review; "write a review and get a discount" with no condition on what it says is allowed).

**Proposed order** (nothing built): the credited upgrade from One Response; the live-deadline clock and a preselected recommended plan on the purchase screens; the early-customer price with a real counter; the case scholarship; Pro annual prepay; then the price test in place of any fee.
