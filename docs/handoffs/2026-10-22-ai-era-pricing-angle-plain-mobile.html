Are our prices too high in the AI world?

Two research agents looked at how sellers use ChatGPT and Claude for appeals, and how AI tools price against free chatbots. Full file: 2026-10-22-ai-era-pricing-angle.md.

Written 22 October 2026 · input to the combined plan

The short answer

  • $249 is fair for what it doesHuman appeal services charge $500 to $3,000.
  • $249 is too much as the first paymentA letter is now free: ChatGPT, AppealsPro's free plan, a free Claude add-on.
  • So: never charge for "a letter"Charge for what a chatbot cannot do: check the seller's documents against what Amazon asked, and remember every round.

What ChatGPT cannot do

"ChatGPT writes the letter. AppealDeck checks the proof."

  • Check each invoice against the noticeDated within 365 days? Right supplier and buyer names? Right products?
  • Add up invoices against units sold"Short by 120 units" is what gets appeals refused.
  • Remember every roundAnd warn when a resubmission is the same as last time.
  • Never invent a factEvery date, number and name comes from the seller.
  • Track deadlines, spot fake notices, build the upload packAll already in the product.

The first payment ($49) is about what people already pay for AI. The full price comes after they have seen a real problem in their own documents.

The professional prices are lower than this morning's plan because writers compare us with AppealsPro at $79.99 a month for about 100 letters. A seller cannot use pro pricing to get one cheap case, because the smallest pack is 10 cases.
